What buyers in this category actually order
Award Medals
Custom Sports & Award Medals

A zinc-alloy die-cast medal, dual-tone gold plating, enamel infill, and a woven or sublimated neck ribbon — this is the highest-volume item in the whole category. It's also the easiest to quietly downgrade on a reorder: a thinner plating pass, a cheaper base alloy, or a printed ribbon swapped in for a woven one, none of which shows up until the piece has been handled a few times.

Standard turnaround is a digital proof in 24–48 hours, then production once the design is locked.

NFC / Smart Tech
NFC-Enabled Coins & Cards

A newer category pairing a die-struck or etched metal coin or card with an embedded NFC chip — tap a phone to it and it opens a digital profile, membership page, or loyalty card, no app required. It's popular for VIP/membership programs and corporate gifting because the card itself never needs to be reprinted when the linked page changes.

Verification here covers more than the metal finish — the chip's read range and write-lock need to be tested against the approved sample, not just assumed to match it.

Enamel & Finish
Hard Enamel, Soft Enamel & Printed Pins

The same pin can be finished five different ways, and each one changes the price and the feel of the piece: hard enamel polished flush with the metal for a glass-like finish, baked enamel for bright saturated colors, soft enamel with a recessed textured feel, full-color offset printing for photo-level detail, and an epoxy dome for a glossy, protective top coat.

Hard enamel is the finish most often quoted and then quietly swapped for the cheaper soft-enamel process once a bulk order goes into production — see the buyer's reference below for exactly what to ask.

Buyer's Reference
The specs that actually separate factories

Most badge, medal, and keychain quotes describe the finished piece in vague terms — "metal," "gold plated," "standard size." The table below turns that vagueness into seven questions you can copy and send to any supplier: a real manufacturer answers immediately with specifics; a reseller hesitates or gives you a vague, reassuring non-answer.

SpecWhat's Commonly OfferedAsk Your Supplier This
Base metalZinc alloy (most common — die-cast for crisp detail) · Iron/steel (budget, can rust if plating wears) · Brass/copper (premium, sharper relief)"What exact alloy are you using — zinc, iron, or brass?"
Plating & colorGold · Antique gold · Silver · Antique silver · Nickel · Black nickel · Copper · Dual-plating (two-tone)"What's the plating thickness in microns, and can you match it to my approved sample?"
Enamel / textureSoft enamel — color recessed below the metal line, slightly concave, visible dividing lines (most common) · Hard enamel / cloisonné — enamel polished flush with the metal, smooth glass-like finish (premium) · Die-struck — no enamel, sculpted metal relief only · Epoxy dome — clear resin coating for extra gloss and scratch protection"Is this soft enamel or hard enamel — can I see a sample of each before I confirm?"
Standard sizesMedals: 50–70mm diameter, 2–3mm thick · Pins & badges: under 32mm (1.25") typically use simple backings; larger pieces need a stronger back"What are the exact millimeters — diameter and thickness?"
Ribbon (medals)20–25mm compact · 30–32mm standard · 35–38mm ceremony-size · Grosgrain (ribbed, matte, best for simple text/logos) or sublimated (full-color print, won't crack or peel)"What's the ribbon width in mm, and is it grosgrain or sublimated?"
Backing (pins/badges)Butterfly clutch (lightweight, most common) · Rubber clutch · Magnetic (no fabric puncture) · Safety pin (for heavier pieces)"Will this backing hold the weight of my design without bending?"
Keychain bodyZinc alloy die-cast · Acrylic / epoxy dome · PVC soft rubber (2D/3D) · Leather-and-metal combination"What exact material is the keychain body made of?"
