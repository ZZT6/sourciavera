01

Distance Makes Verification Impossible

A flight from Guatemala City to Guangzhou costs between $1,000 and $1,700 USD — not counting hotel or the days you'd lose. Most buyers don't even know what to look for once they arrive. We're already here: that's our advantage, and yours.

02

No FTA With China: You Pay Full Duties

Guatemala has no free trade agreement with China: you pay the full import duty (DAI 10% on CIF) plus VAT (12% on CIF+DAI) — in total, close to 23.2% of the CIF value goes to taxes before your container even touches land. A supplier who fails on quality doesn't just ruin the order — it means that 23% is lost too.

03

The Language Barrier Multiplies the Risk

Most suppliers on Alibaba communicate in basic English. In Spanish, communication is practically nonexistent. Every misunderstanding in specifications — dimensions, materials, load capacity, finishes — becomes very expensive once the container is already sailing toward Puerto Barrios or Santo Tomás.

04

Without Verified Evidence, There's No Claim to Make

When a supplier fails, the claim mechanisms from Guatemala are limited. Alibaba's Trade Assurance has deadlines and restrictions. Without independent documentation — photos, video, measurements taken before shipment — you don't have a case. We give you that evidence.

Real Case

A Real Story: Factory Verification for a Guatemalan Client

What happens when you check a factory in person — and what doesn't show up in the sales videos

A few months ago, a Guatemalan client contacted us looking to manufacture expandable container houses for personal use. We'd been working together for weeks — floor plans, quotes, spec adjustments — when the moment that actually matters arrived: verifying the factory in person, before he committed his investment.

He wanted a 16-point audit. We were honest with him: that meant more time, more cost, and probably more than he actually needed for his case. We talked it through point by point until we landed on 8 critical ones — the ones that genuinely protected his decision. We didn't sell him the most expensive package. We helped him find the one he needed.

We drove almost two hours to the factory, in over 34°C (93°F) heat. And there we ran into something we'd rather tell him straight than dress up: the factory didn't have a sample unit assembled to show that day. We could have filmed anything and said everything was perfect. Instead, we explained the real situation and adjusted the plan on the spot — we filmed, point by point, every item on his list: electrical installations, outlets, finishes. Everything he needed to see with his own eyes, not through a salesperson's words.

What we remember most about this case, honestly, isn't the logistics. It's that at some point it stopped feeling like a transaction. There were weeks where everything moved fast — him reviewing every video, every plan, replying the same day. And there was a week where everything stopped completely, while he handled personal matters and coordinated with his partner in Guatemala. We didn't push. We stayed there, at the pace he needed, because a decision of this size — buying something you can't touch, from a country you'll probably never visit — shouldn't be rushed.

This is how we work, on every case:

  • If something isn't ready to show, we tell you — we don't improvise a nicer version of reality
  • We don't sell you the biggest package. We help you decide on the one you actually need
  • We understand that importing from China rarely moves in a straight line — and we match our pace to yours, not the other way around
