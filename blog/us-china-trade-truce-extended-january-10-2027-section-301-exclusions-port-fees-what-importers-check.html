US-China Truce Extended to Jan 10, 2027: Importer Checks

Market News
SourciaVera TeamNov 10, 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer: The United States and China extended their trade truce by two months, to January 10, 2027, according to U.S. and Chinese announcements. USTR extended 178 Section 301 exclusions and the suspension of its maritime action. China kept a 24% tariff on U.S. goods suspended, with a 10% levy still charged.

Verified as of November 10, 2026 against USTR's two notices filed for public inspection on November 9, 2026 (FR Doc. 2026-22925 and FR Doc. 2026-22867), scheduled for publication on November 10, and against Supply Chain Dive and Taiwan's CNA, which report China's announcements. We did not open the Chinese ministries' own pages, so China's side is attributed to those outlets.

This article sets out what each side extended, what the sources do not say and what an importer can check. It does not predict the outcome of the leaders' meeting. It is based on the documents cited and on general good practice, not on any first-hand experience of these measures. Your customs broker or trade attorney decides how any rule applies to your entries.

What Did Each Side Extend?

The U.S. notices say "through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027", and the press describes the pause as running "until January 10, 2027". They are the same moment: January 10 is the first day without the extension. According to Supply Chain Dive, the extension is part of an agreement the two countries reached in September 2026, and the pause on the trade actions would otherwise have ended on Tuesday, November 10, 2026 (Supply Chain Dive).

Why Does It Matter to a U.S. Buyer?

  • The Section 301 exclusions continue. Entries of excluded products made before the new deadline are not charged the additional Section 301 duty; see the Section 301 exclusions article, updated November 10, 2026.
  • The maritime fees and crane duties stay suspended. The notice says the extension allows further discussion with China (Federal Register). Several trade groups are urging USTR to remove the fees permanently, according to Supply Chain Dive (Supply Chain Dive).
  • It is a pause, not a settlement. The measures could return after the new dates, and the sources do not say what happens then.

What Is Still Unknown?

  • What happens after January 10, 2027. We found no statement beyond the new dates.
  • The outcome of the leaders' meeting. According to Reuters, as quoted by other outlets, Trump and Xi are expected to meet at APEC in Shenzhen; the dates are November 18-19, 2026 according to apec.org.
  • China's own texts. We read them only as reported by CNA and Supply Chain Dive.
  • The final Federal Register publication. We read the public-inspection versions; both are scheduled for November 10, 2026.
  • Other duties. These notices do not change other U.S. tariffs, but we did not review them all; ask your broker.

What Should a Buyer Ask the Supplier and the Forwarder to Confirm?

This section is general advice, not a rule from the documents.

  • The expected entry date of each shipment, so you know which entries fall before or after January 9, 2027 (see China-US freight in November 2026).
  • The product description and tariff number the supplier uses, so your broker can check whether an exclusion applies (see the documents to request from a Chinese manufacturer).
  • The price validity and who pays any change in duties or fees, written into the proforma invoice (see what to check on a proforma invoice and which price term to ask a Chinese supplier to quote).
  • Plans for entries after January 10, 2027, such as earlier shipment or a clause that lets you adjust the price.

What Do the Sources Not Tell You?

They do not tell you whether your product is excluded or what duty you would pay if the pause ends. They say nothing about any supplier.

What Should You Do Next?

  1. List your open orders from China with expected entry dates before and after January 9, 2027.
  2. Ask your customs broker whether your products match one of the 178 exclusions.
  3. Write the price validity and duty responsibility into each proforma invoice.
  4. Check the Federal Register and USTR pages after the Shenzhen meetings, before you commit to new orders.
  5. Check any new supplier before you pay, see the supplier verification checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long did the U.S. and China extend their trade truce?

By two months, to January 10, 2027, according to USTR's notices, which run through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027, and to reports of China's announcements by Supply Chain Dive and CNA.

What did USTR extend?

All 178 Section 301 exclusions on Chinese products, through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027, and the suspension of its Section 301 action on China's maritime, logistics and shipbuilding sectors, through the same time.

What did China extend?

As reported, it kept a 24% additional tariff on U.S. goods suspended while still charging a 10% levy, continued suspending October 2025 export controls to January 10, 2027, and suspended special port fees on U.S. ships for two months.

Does this change the tariff on Chinese goods I import?

The notices we read extend exclusions and suspensions; they do not set a new tariff rate. Supply Chain Dive says the reduced 10% fentanyl-related tariff stays. Ask your broker which duties apply to your entries.

What should I do before January 10, 2027?

List open orders by expected entry date, check with your broker whether an exclusion applies, write price validity and duty responsibility into the proforma invoice, and recheck the notices after the APEC meetings.

Summary and Next Step

The U.S.-China pause was extended two months, to January 10, 2027: USTR extended the 178 Section 301 exclusions and the suspension of its maritime action, and China kept its 24% tariff on U.S. goods suspended while charging a 10% levy. The first step is to list your open orders by expected entry date.
