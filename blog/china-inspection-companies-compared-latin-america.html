10 China Inspection Companies Compared for Latin America

By Zhang Peng (Miguel Lopez), Founder · Jun 23, 2026 · Updated Oct 24, 2026 · 14 min read
Notice: this article was written by SourciaVera, which also appears in the list (no. 10). So that you can trust the comparison, we use only public information from each company's official website, we show the sources at the end and we include our own limitations too. Prices change; always confirm them with each company before you hire.

In short: there is no single “best” inspection company for everyone. If you need a certificate from an accredited body or laboratory tests, choose a large or accredited company (SGS, Bureau Veritas, QIMA, HQTS). If you already have a supplier and want inspections with a published rate, V-Trust, QCadvisor and Bureau Veritas InSpec show prices of about 245 to 298 USD per man-day. And if you are a Latin American small or mid-size business that needs people who speak Spanish, show you the factory on video and also help you buy, that is where we fit.

How we compared

We looked at five things that matter to a Latin American buyer:

  1. Published price (whether they show it and whether it is “all-inclusive”).
  2. Spanish and presence in Latin America.
  3. Speed (when the inspector arrives and when the report arrives).
  4. Accreditations and laboratory.
  5. Who each one is best for.

Global giants

1. SGS

One of the largest inspection, verification, testing and certification companies in the world. Its strength is accreditations and laboratories.

  • Best for: when an authority, a bank or a retail chain asks you for a certificate with a recognized name, or when you need laboratory tests.
  • Worth noting: price on quotation; ask for scope, timing and cost in writing.

2. Bureau Veritas (InSpec by BV)

Another global giant. Its online InSpec service publishes a rate from 245 USD per man-day (booking 15 days ahead; glossary), next-day inspection, same-day report, all-inclusive pricing and service 365 days a year.

  • Best for: anyone who wants the backing of a global brand with the convenience of booking online.
  • Worth noting: the lowest price requires booking in advance; check their cancellation and rescheduling terms.

Inspection platforms and companies in Asia

3. QIMA

Founded in Hong Kong in 2005 under the name AsiaInspection (it took the name QIMA in 2018). It is one of the few with a strong physical presence in Mexico: its site speaks of 10 offices and labs in the country, and in 2020 it acquired NYCE, a Mexican standardization and certification body. It offers NOM-compliant testing and certification, and states all-inclusive pricing for product inspections and supplier audits in Mexico.

  • Best for: brands and importers with steady volume, and anyone who also needs help with NOM certification in Mexico.
  • Worth noting: it does not publish a specific rate; ask for the quotation in writing.

4. V-Trust

Founded in 2006, it presents itself as Asia's largest product inspection company and as the first to guarantee that only full-time inspectors are used. It covers 10 Asian countries. It publishes an all-inclusive price: 268 USD per man-day in the highlighted areas and 298 USD elsewhere, with no extra charge for Saturday or Sunday inspections. Its site has a Spanish version.

  • Best for: importers who inspect often and want a fixed, predictable price.
  • Worth noting: confirm with them whether your factory is inside the 268 USD areas and when you will receive the report.

5. HQTS

An inspection, audit and testing company. Its site says it is accredited by CNAS (China's national accreditation body) for ISO 17020 (inspection) and ISO 17025 (testing). The group was founded in Hong Kong in 2009 and has a Spanish site.

  • Best for: anyone who needs inspection from an accredited body and laboratory tests from the same provider.
  • Worth noting: price on quotation; ask for the scope of the accreditation that covers your product.

6. Testcoo

A Chinese online inspection platform. Its site describes ordering online in one minute, same-day quotation and scheduling, inspection the next day and a report in as little as 24 hours. It also offers virtual factory audits with 360-degree VR panoramic cameras. Its site is available in Chinese, English, French and Spanish.

  • Best for: buyers who want to order everything online and get quick answers.
  • Worth noting: it does not publish prices; ask for the quotation and the report contents in writing.

Mid-size specialists

7. Pro QC International

Operating since 1984. Besides inspections it audits quality systems such as ISO 9001 and IATF 16949 (the automotive industry standard). Its China services page lists local offices in Shenzhen, Shanghai and Ningbo, and its site includes a phone line and a services page for Mexico.

  • Best for: auto parts and industrial products where auditing the quality system matters, not just the finished product.
  • Worth noting: price on quotation.

8. QCadvisor

An independent quality control agency headquartered in Hong Kong with offices in Shenzhen. It publishes a rate from 280 USD per man-day across most of Asia, all-inclusive in mainland China, and has a Spanish site for Mexico. Its pre-shipment service for Mexican customs (“Previo en Origen”) checks at the factory, before the container is sealed, NOM compliance, commercial labeling, tariff classifications and quantities. Its reports include photographic evidence. It states that it is not ISO-certified (such as ISO 9001 or ISO 17020), by its own choice.

  • Best for: e-commerce sellers and mid-size brands that want speed and a clear price.
  • Worth noting: if you need a certificate from an accredited body, it is not the option. Outside China or in remote areas, travel expenses may apply.

9. JingSourcing

A sourcing agent in China that also does factory audits. It publishes a price of 200 USD per auditor per day with no extra fees, and offers real-time contact during the audit, including a video call to see the factory. If the factory is not suitable, it can help you find another one, audit it and follow up on production.

  • Best for: small businesses that want audit and sourcing from the same team.
  • Worth noting: its site and content are in English; confirm the current rate directly with them.

Red flags when hiring an inspection service

  1. A very low price with the promise of checking everything in one day. A 2011 article by Renaud Anjoran (Sofeast, published on QualityInspection.org) argues that some agencies with a fixed price for a single man-day rely on photographers rather than inspectors. Ask how many units the inspector will check and how long they will be at the factory.
  2. Photos only, with no date or video. With loose photos it is hard to confirm they are from that factory and that day. Ask for photos with date and time, and a continuous video from the entrance to the production area.
  3. It is not clear who pays them. An article from Cornell's Einaudi Center, featuring professor Sarosh Kuruvilla, deals with bribes, fake factories and forged documents in factory audits in China. Ask directly whether the company receives any payment or commission from factories.
  4. They will not show you a sample report. Before you pay, ask for a real report (with the details hidden) to see what you will receive.

What an inspection in China does not solve (if you import into Mexico)

An inspection or verification in China checks the factory and the goods. It does not replace these procedures in Mexico:

  • NOM: if your product is subject to a Mexican Official Standard (NOM), it needs evaluation by a certification body or verification unit accredited in Mexico, depending on the standard that applies.
  • Tariff classification, permits and taxes: your customs broker determines these.

Before you buy, confirm with your customs broker which requirements apply to your product.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an inspection in China cost in 2026?

Companies that publish a price charge from about 245 to about 300 USD per man-day for a product inspection in China. The large global companies usually quote per project.

What does man-day mean?

One inspector working one day at the factory. An order with many models or a large quantity may need more than one man-day.

What does all-inclusive mean?

That the price already includes the inspector's travel and the report. Some companies charge travel expenses separately when the factory is far away or outside China; always ask.

Do all companies send video?

Not always. What the report includes changes by company and service: some mention photos, others offer a video call or video. If video matters to you, ask for it in writing before you pay.

How do I know the inspector really went to the factory?

Ask for photos with date and time, and a continuous video from the entrance to the production area. At SourciaVera we send you the video of every visit.

Do I need an accredited company?

Only if someone requires a certificate from an accredited body (an authority, a bank or a retail chain) or if your product needs laboratory tests. To check that a factory exists and review the quality of your order, it is not always necessary.

What is the difference between verifying a factory and inspecting an order?

Verification checks the company before you pay: whether it exists, whether it is a factory or an intermediary, what capacity it has. Inspection checks your goods: quantity, quality and packing before shipment.
