9 Alibaba Supplier Red Flags That Should Make You Walk Away

Aug 15, 2026 · 8 min read · Zhang Peng, Founder
Back to Blog

Not every bad Alibaba experience is an outright scam. Sometimes the factory is real but simply wrong for your order — wrong scale, wrong equipment, wrong quality tier. These 9 red flags follow the order you'd actually run into them: first when screening a profile, then during early conversation, then when you get to photos, paperwork, and payment terms. Both fraud and bad-fit factories show up at every stage.

Red Flag #1 — The Profile Was Created Recently

New storefront, aggressive pricing, no order history. Real manufacturers build Alibaba profiles over years — they have transaction history, response time records, and accumulated buyer reviews. A profile created in the last 6 months with no verified orders should be treated as completely unproven, regardless of what their self-written description says.

Red Flag #2 — They Claim to Manufacture Everything

A factory in Guangzhou that makes electronics, furniture, textiles, and automotive parts doesn't exist. Specialization is the rule in Chinese manufacturing — clusters of factories in specific regions specialize in specific product categories. If the product catalog spans 12 unrelated categories, the entity you're dealing with is a trading company sourcing from multiple factories — and they may not vet their own supply chain any more carefully than you're vetting them.

Red Flag #3 — No Verifiable Business Registration

Every legitimate Chinese manufacturer has a business registration (营业执照) you can request. If they won't share it, or the name on it doesn't match their Alibaba profile or payment account, stop. China's National Enterprise Credit Information System allows basic public company lookups — a real factory will be there. The business registration should show manufacturing as the registered business scope. If it shows trading or consulting, you're not dealing with a factory.

Red Flag #4 — They Can't Answer Basic Technical Questions

Ask something product-specific: "What alloy grade do you use for this bracket?" "What LED chip brand do you source?" "What lumen output tolerance do you hold in production?" A factory that manufactures your product answers these immediately and specifically. A trading company that resells it has to "check with the factory" — which tells you exactly where you sit in the supply chain, and how much visibility you'll have if something goes wrong.

Red Flag #5 — Slow Response During China Business Hours

A real factory's sales team handles inquiries during Guangzhou working hours (UTC+8, 9am–6pm Monday–Friday). If you consistently get replies only outside those hours, or never within a 4-hour window during business hours, the seller may be a part-time operation, a reseller in a different timezone, or someone running multiple storefronts from a laptop. Speed and responsiveness during business hours is one of the few things you can verify from a screen.

Red Flag #6 — Factory Photos Are Stock or Appear on Multiple Profiles

A factory proud of their operation will send you photos of their actual floor — specific machines, specific workers, specific products in progress. If you reverse-image-search the photos provided and find them on multiple supplier storefronts, on competitor profiles, or on a stock photo site — the "factory" you're looking at doesn't match the operation you'd be sending money to. Ask for a live walkthrough video at a specific time instead.

Red Flag #7 — MOQ Is Much Lower Than Industry Standard

Minimum order quantities exist because production runs have real setup costs. If the industry standard is 500 units and this supplier will do 10, one of two things is happening: they have overstock they're liquidating, or they're not actually manufacturing. Low MOQ can be a genuine feature in some contexts — or a signal that the "factory" is a trader with no production floor.

Red Flag #8 — No Written Contract, Just Chat

If the supplier resists putting order terms in writing on the Alibaba platform, they're avoiding the documentation trail that Trade Assurance needs to process a dispute. Legitimate suppliers have no reason to avoid documentation — it protects them too. Insist on a formal order confirmation with product specifications, quantity, quality standards, packaging requirements, and delivery timeline. If they won't do it, walk.

Red Flag #9 — They Create Artificial Urgency Around Payment

"Prices increase next week." "We only have capacity for one more buyer this quarter." "Our manager approved this deal, but only until Friday." Real urgency in manufacturing exists — production slots and material pricing do fluctuate. But urgency tied to a payment deadline from an unverified supplier is almost always pressure-based manipulation designed to bypass your due diligence process.

What to Do With This List

These flags don't always mean fraud. Some mean inexperience. Some mean mismatched scale. But any two of them together on a supplier you're evaluating is either a clear reason to walk away, or a clear reason to get eyes on the factory in person before committing funds.

Due diligence is not a sign of distrust — it's what every experienced importer does before every significant order. The factories that welcome it are the ones worth working with.
