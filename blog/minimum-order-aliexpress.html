What would be the minimum amount of product that should be purchased from AliExpress?

By Zhang Peng (Miguel Lopez), Founder · Aug 31, 2026 · Updated Oct 16, 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer: AliExpress's own pages describe no platform-wide minimum order. It is a retail platform and each seller and listing decides: some set a minimum quantity or amount, others do not. So the minimum is whatever the listing and checkout show. One or two units show you a product, not what a bulk order will look like.

If you are asking this, you are probably choosing between a tiny test order and a bigger one, and you don't want a bare "it depends" without a way to check. Here is how to check, and when AliExpress stops being the right tool.

Does AliExpress Have a Minimum Order?

Alibaba Group describes AliExpress as a global retail e-commerce platform that lets consumers buy directly from manufacturers around the world (Alibaba Group, read Oct 8, 2026). AliExpress's own page on minimum purchases says sellers can customize their minimum purchase policies, for example by quantity or by amount, and does not describe a platform-wide minimum (AliExpress, undated, read Oct 8, 2026). We did not find an official AliExpress page that sets one.

So there is no number to memorize. Check the listing instead:

  • The quantity box and any note about a minimum on the product page and at checkout.
  • The unit the price refers to: a piece, a set or a pack. The same trap applies as on Alibaba.com; see the difference between "set" and "piece".
  • The total delivered price, with shipping, and any duties and taxes your country charges.

What Does AliExpress Buyer Protection Cover?

According to AliExpress's Buyer Protection page, you are covered for 15 days from receiving the items if the order does not match the description or photos, or arrived damaged or short, and also if it did not arrive within the guaranteed delivery window. The page lists exclusions, including prohibited items, underwear, socks, food, batteries and customized items, and says the rules differ for some countries. A custom order is not covered in the same way as a standard one.

How Many Should You Buy?

  • For yourself: the quantity you need. There is no reason to buy more to reach a number.
  • To test a product idea: one or two units are enough to look at the product, check the finish and the packaging, and time the delivery. They do not tell you what 1,000 units will look like. A sample proves that the factory can make your product once, by hand, when it knows you are watching, and the same limit applies to a single unit you bought online (Sample Looks Great, Bulk Order Is Garbage).
  • To resell: check the margin before the quantity. Our dropshipping platforms guide and dropshipping with Alibaba guide cover the platform side.
  • Hundreds of identical units, a custom specification, your own branding or packaging: this is where a retail listing stops fitting. Ask suppliers for prices at three quantities, as in our MOQ guide, or read how to buy wholesale from a factory.

Who Are You Actually Buying From?

A retail listing does not tell you whether the seller makes the product. For one item for yourself, that rarely changes your decision. For a product you plan to sell, it does: a factory, a trading company and a reseller can quote very different prices and quantities. I don't mind a trader, and I don't automatically trust a factory. What I care about is that the buyer knows which one they're paying, and why (Trading Company vs Factory).

What Should You Do Next?

  1. Open the listing and check the quantity box, any note about a minimum, and the unit the price refers to.
  2. Read the Buyer Protection terms that apply to your item and your country: 15 days from receiving the items, with some items excluded.
  3. Compare the delivered price with other options if you need more than a few units. Our guides to buying a single product from Alibaba and to what happens when you ask a supplier for one item show how to ask for one unit and how to read the reply.
  4. If you are testing a product for a business, buy one or two units, write down what you check, and remember that it does not predict bulk quality.
  5. If you need volume or customization, ask suppliers for prices at 50, 100 and 300 units and compare them as landed cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a minimum order on AliExpress?

We did not find an official AliExpress page that sets a platform-wide minimum order. AliExpress's own page on minimum purchases says sellers can customize their policies, for example by quantity or by amount, so the minimum is whatever the listing and the checkout show.

Can I buy just one item on AliExpress?

It depends on the listing. Alibaba Group describes AliExpress as a retail platform for consumers and each seller sets its own policy, so check the quantity box and any minimum noted on the listing before you pay.

How does AliExpress Buyer Protection work?

According to AliExpress's Buyer Protection page, you are covered for 15 days from receiving the items if the order does not match the description or photos or arrived damaged or short, and if it did not arrive within the guaranteed delivery window. Some items are excluded.

Does Buyer Protection cover customized products?

AliExpress's Buyer Protection page lists customized items among the exclusions, so a custom order is not covered in the same way.

Is AliExpress or Alibaba.com better for small quantities?

Alibaba Group builds AliExpress for consumers and Alibaba.com for business buyers. For a few units for yourself, a retail platform is the natural fit; for volume, customization or negotiating with a supplier, compare Alibaba.com suppliers. Compare the total landed price either way.

Summary and Next Step

AliExpress has no single minimum that we could find; the listing is the answer. Read the unit, read the protection terms, compare the delivered price, and match the quantity to the reason you are buying. You don't have to know everything about Chinese manufacturing before you buy from China. A single unit from a retail listing is a fine way to look at a product, as long as you remember how little it tells you about the factory behind it.

In short, the listing is the answer: read the unit and the protection terms and match the quantity to your reason for buying. If you later move from one retail unit to a real order, the realistic risk is trusting a listing that says little about the factory behind it.
