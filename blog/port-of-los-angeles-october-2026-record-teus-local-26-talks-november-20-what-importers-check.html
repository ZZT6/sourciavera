LA Port Sets October Record; Strike Talks Run to Nov 20

Market News
SourciaVera TeamNov 12, 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer: The Port of Los Angeles handled a record 1,026,872 TEUs in October 2026, and its watchmen's contract talks were extended to November 20. A strike by about 250 watchmen could disrupt terminals that handle 33% of Los Angeles cargo. Plan for delays on imports.

Verified as of November 12, 2026 against the Port of Los Angeles' announcement as carried by Random Lengths News and MyNewsLA, against gCaptain, and against Reuters and Sourcing Journal on the labor talks. We did not open the port's own web page, so the figures are attributed to those outlets.

This article sets out what the port reported, what is at stake in the labor talks and what an importer can ask the forwarder and the supplier to confirm. It does not predict whether there will be a strike. It is based on the sources cited and on general good practice, not on any first-hand experience of these ports. Your forwarder and customs broker decide how it applies to your shipments.

What Did the Port Report for October 2026?

October 2026 figures of the Port of Los Angeles, as announced on November 12, 2026. Read November 12, 2026.
MeasureFigure
Total containers1,026,872 TEUs: a record for October and the second-highest month ever
Previous recordSeptember 2026, 1,042,652 TEUs; the first time the port passed 1 million TEUs in consecutive months
Loaded importsAbout 538,620 TEUs, up 25% from October 2025 and 30% above the five-year October average
Loaded exports114,981 TEUs, down 7% from October 2025
Empty containers373,271 TEUs, a second straight monthly record, as carriers move equipment back to Asia
January to OctoberAbout 9,108,497 TEUs, 5% ahead of last year and 9% above the five-year average

The port's executive director, Gene Seroka, attributed the volume to consumer spending, retailers replenishing inventories, changes in trade policy and disruptions on other shipping routes, and said November volumes also look strong (Random Lengths News; gCaptain). According to gCaptain, he said the cargo includes parts, components and equipment for U.S. businesses as well as consumer goods, and that Chinese factories he visited are running at full capacity, with importers expected to speed up shipments before Lunar New Year (gCaptain).

What Is the Strike Risk?

  • Who and when. The Pacific Maritime Association and ILWU Local 26, which represents about 250 maritime watchmen, extended their bargaining deadline through November 20, 2026, according to Reuters. The deadline had moved from November 1 to November 4, then to November 6 (Reuters; Sourcing Journal).
  • What a strike would hit. The PMA said a watchmen strike would disrupt terminals that handle roughly 85% of the cargo at Long Beach and 33% at Los Angeles (Reuters). The watchmen control and inspect truck and employee entries at terminal gates and provide terminal security.
  • Why it could spread. According to gCaptain, other ILWU locals, including longshore workers in Local 13 and marine clerks in Local 63, have indicated they would honor Local 26 picket lines (gCaptain).
  • Status of the talks. The watchmen have worked under extensions since their contract expired in July 2025, and the PMA said there had been no progress on a deal despite regular meetings; the union has not commented publicly (Sourcing Journal). The watchmen's contract is separate from the six-year dockworker agreement reached in 2023.

What About Rail and Trucking Delays?

According to gCaptain, about 9,500 rail-bound import containers at Los Angeles had waited nine days or longer for on-dock rail, and another 5,063 at Long Beach faced similar delays; at Los Angeles about 67% of containers left terminals within four days, 11% stayed five to eight days and 23% stayed nine days or longer. C.H. Robinson cited a shortage of intermodal railcars in Southern California, and an ITS Logistics executive said shippers should be prepared to add trucking capacity through Lunar New Year (gCaptain). For the trade-policy side, see the U.S.-China pause extended to January 10, 2027, which keeps the Section 301 port fees on Chinese vessels suspended.

What Is Still Unknown?

  • Whether there will be a deal, another extension or a strike after November 20, 2026.
  • How long a strike would last and how many terminals would close; the figures above are the PMA's description of what is at stake.
  • The port's own text. We read it through outlets that carried the announcement.
  • Long Beach's October figures. We did not find them in the sources we read.

What Should a Buyer Ask the Supplier and the Forwarder to Confirm?

This section is general advice, not a statement from the sources.

  • The vessel, the arrival port and the expected discharge date, and how the forwarder will track container dwell time (see China-US freight in November 2026).
  • How the container will leave the terminal: truck or rail, who books it and what the plan is if rail is delayed.
  • Which charges apply if the container waits, such as storage, detention or demurrage, and who pays under your price term (see which price term to ask a Chinese supplier to quote).
  • The supplier's cargo-ready date, written on the proforma invoice, so booking and production match (see what to check on a proforma invoice).
  • An alternative gateway or a later sailing if the forwarder expects a stoppage around November 20.

What Do the Sources Not Tell You?

They do not tell you how your shipment will be affected or what your forwarder will charge. They say nothing about any supplier or carrier.

What Should You Do Next?

  1. List your shipments due at Los Angeles and Long Beach through the end of November with their expected discharge dates.
  2. Ask your forwarder about dwell times, the inland move and the plan if talks fail on November 20.
  3. Write the cargo-ready date and who pays storage and detention into the proforma invoice.
  4. Plan trucking or rail early for containers that will arrive in the second half of November.
  5. Check any new supplier before you pay, see the supplier verification checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many containers did the Port of Los Angeles handle in October 2026?

1,026,872 TEUs, a record for October and the second-highest month ever, according to the port's announcement of November 12, 2026. Loaded imports were about 538,620 TEUs, up 25% from October 2025.

Is there a strike at the Los Angeles and Long Beach ports?

Not as of November 12, 2026. The PMA and ILWU Local 26, which represents about 250 watchmen, extended their bargaining deadline through November 20, 2026, according to Reuters. A strike remains possible if no deal is reached.

How much cargo would a watchmen strike affect?

The PMA said it would disrupt terminals that handle roughly 85% of the cargo at Long Beach and 33% at Los Angeles, according to Reuters. gCaptain adds that other ILWU locals have indicated they would honor picket lines.

Are there other delays at the ports?

According to gCaptain, about 9,500 rail-bound import containers at Los Angeles had waited nine days or longer, and Long Beach had 5,063 in a similar position. C.H. Robinson cited a shortage of intermodal railcars.

What should I ask my forwarder?

How the container will leave the terminal, how dwell time is tracked, who pays storage and detention under your price term, and what the alternative is if a stoppage starts around November 20.
