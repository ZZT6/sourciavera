8 Documents You Should Always Request From a Chinese Manufacturer

Sep 4, 2026 · 9 min read · Zhang Peng
Back to Blog

Most buyers know to "ask for documents" before paying a deposit. Few know which ones actually prove anything. Some of the 8 below any trading company can produce in five minutes. Others only mean something if someone is physically standing in the factory to confirm they're real — that split is marked next to each one, so you know exactly what you can check yourself and where you actually need boots on the ground.

1. Business License (营业执照)

Every legitimate Chinese manufacturer has one. The registered business scope should say "manufacturing" — not "trading" or "consulting" — for the category you're buying.

You Can Check

Ask for a photo of the license and read the registered business scope line yourself.

What We Verify On-Site

We cross-check the license number against China's National Enterprise Credit system and confirm the registered address matches the actual factory.

2. Export License or Export Agent Agreement

Not every real factory holds its own export license — some route shipments through a licensed agent, which is normal. What matters is knowing which one you're paying.

You Can Check

Ask directly whether they export themselves or through an agent, and get the agent's name if so.

What We Verify On-Site

We confirm the export paperwork used on your actual shipment matches the entity you're paying — not a third party inserted at the last step.

3. Third-Party Factory Audit Report

A factory proud of its operation usually has a recent audit — SGS, Bureau Veritas, or independent — and will share the report number without hesitation.

You Can Check

Ask if an audit exists from the last 12 months and request the report number.

What We Verify On-Site

If no recent audit exists, we run one — production line count, equipment, and workforce headcount confirmed in person, not from a brochure.

4. Product Test Reports and Certifications

CE, RoHS, FCC, DOT — whatever your destination market requires — should exist for the exact product model, not a similar one from the same catalog.

You Can Check

Ask which lab issued the certificate and for the certificate number.

What We Verify On-Site

We confirm the certificate number is real and matches the exact model you're ordering.

5. National Enterprise Credit System Record

China's public credit registry (gsxt.gov.cn) shows registered capital, business scope, and any flagged irregularities — for anyone who can read it.

You Can Check

You can run a basic lookup yourself using the company's Chinese legal name.

What We Verify On-Site

We interpret what the record actually shows in context — irregularities that aren't obvious if you don't read Chinese regulatory filings regularly.

6. Dated, Unedited Factory Photos

Stock catalog photos tell you nothing. A photo with today's date visible tells you the factory exists right now, as described.

You Can Check

Ask for a photo with today's date written on paper and held up in frame.

What We Verify On-Site

We take our own timestamped, geotagged photos and video during the visit, so you have evidence you didn't have to ask for.

7. Sealed Sample Confirmation Sheet

Without a written record of exactly what was approved, "the bulk order doesn't match the sample" becomes impossible to prove either way.

You Can Check

Insist on a signed sheet describing material, color, and dimensions of the approved sample.

What We Verify On-Site

We compare the bulk production line against the physical sealed sample — not a photo of it.

8. Formal Sales Contract or Proforma Invoice

Specifications, quantity, quality standard, packaging, and delivery date — in writing, before any payment leaves your account.

You Can Check

Get every term above written into the order confirmation, not just agreed to in chat.

What We Verify On-Site

We review the contract in both languages and flag ambiguous clauses before you sign, not after a dispute starts.

What This List Actually Protects You From

None of these 8 documents guarantee a perfect order. What they do is remove the excuses — "the factory says," "we'll check and get back to you," "that got lost in translation" — that show up after money has already moved. A supplier with nothing to hide produces all 8 without friction. One that stalls on two or more of them is telling you something, whether or not they say it out loud.
