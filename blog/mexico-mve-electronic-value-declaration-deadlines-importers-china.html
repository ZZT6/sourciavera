Mexico's MVE Customs Value Filing: Dates and What to Fix

By Zhang Peng (Miguel Lopez), Founder · Oct 21, 2026 · Updated Oct 24, 2026 · 9 min read

The short answer: October 31, 2026 ends a transition period; it is not one deadline for everyone. From November 1, filing the electronic customs value declaration (MVE) becomes mandatory in stages, by customs regime, and definitive imports start on January 15, 2027. Until then, make your supplier's invoice, contract and payment tell the same story.

Verified as of October 24, 2026 against the first advance version of the SAT's Third Resolution, as published on the SAT website, and the Diario Oficial; what comes from the press or from law-firm notes is marked "according to".

This article covers what the reports say, what has to match and what to agree with your Chinese supplier now. It is based on the SAT's advance text of the rule, the Diario Oficial, two law-firm notes, one press report and general good practice, not on any first-hand experience of these filings. Your customs broker and the official texts decide how it applies to you.

Why Does the MVE Matter to a Buyer?

  • It puts the price in a system. The MVE is the electronic transmission of the data used to determine the customs value of the goods; a law-firm guide describes it as generating a 13-character folio that links to the pedimento (the electronic customs entry filed for each shipment; glossary) (Simetria Legal).
  • The importer carries the responsibility. The Customs Law already requires the importer to give its broker, under oath, the elements that allow the value to be determined (art. 59, fraction III), and a service guide says the responsibility for the data and its transmission lies with the importer, not the broker (Facturando).
  • Mismatches become visible. The same law-firm guide lists what must agree: the invoice, the pedimento, the contract terms, the bank transfers and their dates, and any added costs. The value questions raised by Mexico's Customs Law reform on goods declared below their value come back through the same documents.

What Are the New Dates?

The SAT's advance text of the Third Resolution of Modifications to the 2026 General Foreign Trade Rules (RGCE) changes the eleventh transitory provision. Until October 31, 2026, those who bring goods into Mexico can keep complying with article 59, fraction III of the Customs Law under the earlier transitory scheme. From November 1 the value declaration has to be transmitted electronically (rule 1.5.1), and the start date depends on the customs regime (SAT advance text). According to Holland & Knight, the SAT published it on September 30, 2026, and the SAT, the National Customs Agency (ANAM) and the Digital Transformation and Telecommunications Agency (ATDT) confirmed the extension in a joint statement on October 2. Garrigues and UnoTV report the same calendar.

Customs regimeElectronic MVE mandatory from
Processing, transformation or repair in a bonded facility (elaboración, transformación o reparación en recinto fiscalizado)November 1, 2026
Strategic bonded facility (recinto fiscalizado estratégico)November 15, 2026

Bonded warehouse (depósito fiscal)December 15, 2026

Definitive import (importación definitiva)January 15, 2027

A typical commercial purchase from China is a definitive import, which starts last, on January 15, 2027. Ask your customs broker which regime applies to each of your shipments and what applies to them until then.

Where is it filed? The RGCE call the "Ventanilla Digital" the Single Window for Foreign Trade Procedures (VUTCE), enabled by a presidential decree published in the Diario Oficial on May 4, 2026 (decree, RGCE definition). The decree says the window's technological operation runs through the Mexican Digital Window of Foreign Trade, also called VUCEM, which is to be transferred from the SAT to the ATDT. So you will see both names: VUTCE is the window and VUCEM is the system it works through.

Until October 31, the transitory scheme still applies, including the traditional E2 value format. Some facilities stay: transport documents, the certificate of origin and the guarantee document need not be transmitted with the MVE if they are attached to the pedimento, and a format with general contract information can replace the transmission of each contract (UnoTV). That contract format, called E15, is described as optional until December 31, 2026 (GBI Trade & Law); we did not find that point in the SAT text.

What Has to Match Between Supplier, Invoice and Payment?

This section is general advice based on the items the guides list. Think of one price story that every document tells in the same way.

  • Invoice, contract and payment. The amount on the commercial invoice, the price in the contract or proforma invoice and the money that leaves your account should agree, including the date of payment. If a discount or a rebate was agreed, put it in writing before the shipment, not after.
  • The price term. What the price includes, freight and insurance in particular, changes the value to declare (see which price term to ask a Chinese supplier to quote). Write the Incoterm and the place on the PI.
  • Added costs. Freight, insurance, commissions, royalties or packing that are not in the price still have to be accounted for in the value. Ask the supplier to show them on separate lines.
  • The contract data. The E15 format (Annex 1 of the RGCE) carries the general data of the contracts behind a transaction; under the Second Resolution published in the DOF on September 22, 2026, until December 31, 2026 an importer may transmit it, declared under oath, instead of each contract. A written contract or PI that states the date, the parties, the goods, the amount, payment terms and currency clearly makes this easier.
  • The same description and code. Use one description of the goods across documents (see HS codes and Mexican tariff fractions).
  • The payer and the payee. The account that is paid should be in the name of the company on the invoice (see how to pay a Chinese supplier).

Where Does a Third-Party Check Help?

It cannot file the MVE or tell you how the authority will read a value. It can check that the story the documents tell is true before the shipment: who is behind the supplier, whether the company on the invoice is the one that receives the payment, whether the price is in line with other quotes and whether the goods are what the invoice says. What Is Still Unknown?

  • The final text. The calendar comes from the SAT's first advance version of the Third Resolution. As of October 24, 2026 we did not find the Third Resolution in the Diario Oficial, so the published text could differ.
  • Whether the dates move again. They have moved before: Garrigues says the MVE was first due on December 9, 2025.
  • Regime by regime details. The text we read gives start dates only. It does not say how each regime will work in practice or what happens to a shipment whose MVE fails.
  • How the authority will review a value. The text does not cover it.

What Does the Reported Change Not Tell You?

It does not tell you how the authority will review a given value, or what penalties apply to a given mismatch. One law-firm guide warns of fines of up to 150% for undervaluation and of precautionary seizure, but we have not checked those figures against the law, so ask your broker. The dates may also change again, as they have before.

What Should You Do Next?

Four questions for your customs broker: which date applies to your regime, whether the MVE is already being transmitted for your shipments, who signs and transmits it for your company, and which documents you must keep for an audit.

  1. Ask your customs broker which date applies to your regime and whether the MVE is already being transmitted for your shipments.
  2. Ask who signs and transmits the MVE in your case, and which documents your broker needs from you to prepare it.
  3. Line up invoice, contract and payment with your supplier for every open order, including freight, insurance and discounts.
  4. Put the price term and the added costs in writing on the PI.
  5. Check the supplier before you pay, and keep the payment record with the invoice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the MVE in Mexican customs?

The Manifestación de Valor Electrónica is the electronic transmission, through the foreign trade single window (VUTCE, which works through the system called VUCEM), of the data used to determine the customs value of imported goods. A law-firm guide says it generates a 13-character folio linked to the pedimento.

Is October 31, 2026 the MVE deadline for everyone?

No. October 31, 2026 ends the transition period. According to the SAT's advance text, from November 1 the electronic MVE becomes mandatory in stages by customs regime: November 1, November 15, December 1 and December 15, 2026, January 1, 2027, and January 15, 2027 for definitive imports (as of October 24, 2026).

Does the importer or the customs broker file the MVE?

A service guide says the responsibility for the data and its transmission lies with the importer, not the broker. Confirm with your broker who signs and transmits it in your case.

What should the supplier's invoice and my payment look like?

They should agree with the contract or proforma invoice on amount, price term and payment date, with freight, insurance and any added costs shown separately. Keep the payment record with the invoice.

Can a third-party check file or approve my MVE?

No. It can check who is behind the supplier, whether the payment account matches the company and whether the goods match the invoice, which helps the documents tell the same story.

Summary and Next Step

October 31, 2026 ends the transition. According to the SAT's advance text, the electronic MVE becomes mandatory in stages from November 1, and for definitive imports on January 15, 2027 (as of October 24, 2026). The change does not create a new risk by itself: it makes an old one visible, the price that does not match across documents. What to prepare is a price that reads the same on every document, in writing, backed by a payment that matches it.
