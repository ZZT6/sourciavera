CBP Foreign Importer of Record Rule: CTPAT by Jan 5, 2027

Market News
SourciaVera TeamNov 8, 2026 · 9 min read

The short answer: CBP's interim final rule of November 6, 2026 requires foreign importers of record to be CTPAT validated or use a validated broker. It takes effect January 5, 2027, and comments are open until the same date. Brokers must also verify each client's U.S. status.

Verified as of November 8, 2026 against the rule in the Federal Register (91 FR 69977, document 2026-22735). We read its explanation in full; we did not read the amendatory regulatory text line by line, so the section numbers below come from the explanation.

This article covers what the rule says, what is still unknown and what to ask your supplier, your forwarder and your broker. It is based on the rule and on general good practice, not on any first-hand experience of its application. Your customs broker or trade attorney decides how it applies to your entries.

Why Does It Matter to a U.S. Buyer?

  • It decides who may be the importer of record. The importer of record is the party legally responsible for the entry and the duties. The rule separates a "U.S. importer of record" from a "foreign importer of record" and adds conditions for the second (Federal Register).
  • The date is close. It is effective January 5, 2027 (Federal Register).
  • It affects delivered-price sales. If a foreign seller acts as the importer of record for your goods, that seller falls under the new conditions, so ask who is named on the entry.

What Does the Rule Say?

Main points of the interim final rule published November 6, 2026. Read November 8, 2026.
ItemWhat the rule says
Effective dateJanuary 5, 2027; comments due the same day (docket USCBP-2026-1387)
BasisSection 2 of Executive Order 14411, "Strengthening Customs Enforcement", signed June 3, 2026
U.S. importer of recordA U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident, or an entity organized under U.S. law and located in the United States that keeps at all times either at least 51% of its beneficial owners as U.S. citizens or permanent residents with control, or direct ownership of a significant amount of unencumbered U.S. real property (new 19 CFR 110.2)
Foreign importer of recordAny importer of record that does not meet those criteria (new 19 CFR 110.3)
New condition for foreign importersMay not enter merchandise for consumption unless it is a CTPAT validated importer of record or designates a CTPAT validated customs broker (new 19 CFR 141.21)
New declarationA U.S. importer of record must declare its qualification when it files the entry and the entry summary (new 19 CFR 141.22)
Broker dutiesVerify and declare that the client qualifies as a U.S. importer of record, and keep records of that verification (amended 19 CFR 111.29, 111.21 and 111.23)
Consequence of a wrong declarationCBP may require a "live entry" (amended 19 CFR 142.13); the penalty guidelines for false statements under 19 U.S.C. 1592 are also revised
Broker timingBrokers may ask for delayed compliance by January 5, 2027; all brokers are expected to comply by May 6, 2027

CBP explains that it will verify U.S. status by looking at substance as well as form, for example whether the entity has its main place of business and a physical presence in the United States, enough unencumbered domestic assets and enough capital, and whether it is an instrumentality of a foreign manufacturer (Federal Register). CBP may ask for records such as proof of citizenship, corporate charters, financial statements and documents of title. The rule defines a beneficial owner as a natural person who exercises substantial control or owns or controls at least 25% of the entity, directly or through other entities (Federal Register).

The executive order also called for a ban on informal entries by foreign importers of record. The rule does not impose that ban, but says CBP will monitor and may prohibit them later, and it invites comments on that alternative (Federal Register).

What Is Still Unknown?

  • How long CTPAT validation takes for a foreign importer that applies after November 6, 2026. We did not find that in the rule.
  • How CBP will decide "significant" real property or "sufficient" assets and capital. The criteria are described as flexible and non-exhaustive.
  • Whether the final text will change. This is an interim final rule with a comment period, so it may be revised.
  • Whether informal entries will be banned for foreign importers. CBP has not decided.
  • How your own entries are affected. That depends on who is named as the importer of record and on whether your company meets the U.S. criteria.

What Should a Buyer Ask the Supplier, the Forwarder and the Broker to Confirm?

This section is general advice, not a rule from the document.

  • Who is the importer of record on each shipment: your company, your broker or the seller. With delivered terms, ask the supplier or forwarder in writing (see which price term to ask a Chinese supplier to quote).
  • If the importer of record is not a U.S. entity, ask whether it is CTPAT validated or uses a CTPAT validated broker, and from which date, so you can compare it with January 5, 2027.
  • If your company is the importer of record, check with your broker whether you meet the U.S. criteria and which records you can show: ownership, premises, assets and capital (see the importer-of-record role in IEEPA refunds for why the named party matters).
  • Your open orders and their expected entry dates, so you know which entries fall on or after January 5, 2027.
  • Who pays any extra cost of the new conditions, written into the proforma invoice (see what to check on a proforma invoice).

What Does the Rule Not Tell You?

It does not tell you whether your company qualifies, which of your entries are affected or what it will cost. It says nothing about any supplier or broker.

What Should You Do Next?

  1. List your open orders from China with expected entry dates and the named importer of record.
  2. Ask your customs broker whether the importer of record meets the U.S. criteria or is CTPAT validated.
  3. Write the responsibility for the entry into the proforma invoice and the price term.
  4. Check the Federal Register and CBP's pages before January 5, 2027 for guidance, FAQs and changes.
  5. Check any new supplier before you pay, see the supplier verification checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did CBP announce on November 6, 2026 about foreign importers of record?

An interim final rule: a foreign importer of record may not enter merchandise for consumption unless it is CTPAT validated or designates a CTPAT validated customs broker. U.S. importers must declare their qualification, and brokers must verify it.

When does the rule take effect?

January 5, 2027, according to the Federal Register. Comments are due the same day. Brokers may request delayed compliance by January 5, 2027 and are expected to comply fully by May 6, 2027.

Who counts as a U.S. importer of record under the rule?

A U.S. citizen or permanent resident, or an entity organized under U.S. law and located in the United States that keeps at least 51% U.S. citizen or permanent resident beneficial owners with control, or owns significant unencumbered U.S. real property.

Does the rule ban informal entries by foreign importers?

No. The executive order called for it, but the rule does not impose it. CBP says it will monitor informal entries and may prohibit them later, and it invites comments on that alternative.

What should I ask my supplier and broker?

Who is the importer of record on each shipment, whether a non-U.S. importer is CTPAT validated or uses a validated broker and from which date, and whether your own company meets the U.S. criteria and can document it.

Summary and Next Step

CBP's rule of November 6, 2026 requires a foreign importer of record to be CTPAT validated or use a validated broker from January 5, 2027, and makes brokers verify a client's U.S. status. The first step is to find out who is the importer of record on each of your open orders.
