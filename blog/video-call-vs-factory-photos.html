5 Things a Live Video Call Can Reveal That Factory Photos Can't

Sep 4, 2026 · 6 min read · Zhang Peng
Back to Blog

Photos can be staged, borrowed from a competitor, or years out of date. A live video call is harder to fake — but only if you use it to actually check something, instead of just nodding along to a screen tour. These 5 checks turn a video call from small talk into real evidence.

1. Whether the Scale Matches the Claim

A "200-worker factory" should visibly have workstations, not one busy corner filmed at a flattering angle.

You Can Check

On the call, ask them to walk to the far end of the production floor and count visible workstations as they go.

What We Verify On-Site

We physically walk the same floor and count equipment against the claimed capacity, no camera angle to manage.

2. Whether Workers and Machines Match the Product Category

A factory that makes your product should be able to point a camera at a machine actually producing it — not a generic assembly line.

You Can Check

Ask them to show the specific machine or line producing your product type, live.

What We Verify On-Site

We identify equipment by make and model and confirm it's actually suited to your product's manufacturing process.

3. Proof It's Not Recorded Footage

Pre-recorded "live tours" are common enough that a simple test is worth running every time.

You Can Check

Ask them to hold up a handwritten note with today's date and a word you choose on the spot.

What We Verify On-Site

We record dated video during every visit as standard evidence included in your report.

4. How the Rep Handles Unscripted Questions

Sales pitches are rehearsed. Real production knowledge shows up when a question falls outside the script.

You Can Check

Ask something not in their usual pitch — a defect rate, a recent production issue, a technical spec.

What We Verify On-Site

We ask harder questions in Mandarin, in person, where evasive answers are easier to catch than through a translated video call.

5. Whether Inventory Matches "Ships Immediately" Claims

"In stock, ready to ship" should be provable in about ten seconds on a video call.

You Can Check

Ask them to walk to the actual warehouse shelf holding your product — not a saved photo they already have.

What We Verify On-Site

We check physical stock counts against what's promised in the listing before you commit to a ship date.

Why This Matters More Than It Seems

None of these 5 checks require special equipment or fluent Mandarin — just a willingness to ask for something specific instead of accepting a general tour. Suppliers who fake video calls are betting that buyers won't push past the polite version. Pushing past it costs you nothing and tells you almost everything.
