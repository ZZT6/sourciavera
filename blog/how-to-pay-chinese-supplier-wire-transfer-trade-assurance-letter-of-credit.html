How to Pay a Chinese Supplier: Wire Transfer, Trade Assurance or Letter of Credit — What Each One Protects

Buyer's Guide
SourciaVera TeamOct 16, 2026 · Updated Oct 24, 2026 · 11 min read

The short answer: no payment method makes a Chinese supplier safe, but each one decides who holds your money and what counts as evidence. A wire transfer (T/T) leaves you with the contract and the supplier's word; Trade Assurance helps only if you pay on Alibaba.com; a letter of credit protects you only as far as the documents you ask for.

If you are about to send a deposit and the supplier has given you bank details, this is the moment to decide how the money will move, before you decide how much.

Why Does the Payment Method Matter?

Three reasons, all about what happens if something goes wrong:

  • Who holds the money while nothing has arrived. With a direct transfer the supplier has it from day one. With an online order on Alibaba.com or a documentary credit, a third party sits between you and the supplier.
  • Who you can ask for help. A platform dispute, a bank claim and a contract dispute are three different routes, each with its own time limits.
  • What evidence counts. Alibaba says its protection rests on your online order and your messages with the supplier; a bank under a letter of credit looks at documents; a wire transfer rests on whatever you have in writing, such as the proforma invoice.

What Does a Direct Wire Transfer (T/T) Give You?

A wire transfer to the supplier's bank account is simple and common, and it gives you the least built-in protection: once the money has arrived, the supplier has it, and your recourse is the contract, your written messages and, in a narrow window, your bank. Our guide to what to do after being scammed on Alibaba explains why a bank recall has to be requested within a short time. If you pay by wire, the name on the account and the payment triggers become your main controls: the beneficiary should be the company you verified, and each payment should be tied to an event written on the PI, not to a date or a request for urgency.

What Does Alibaba Trade Assurance Cover, and What Must You Do?

According to Alibaba's buyer page, Trade Assurance applies to orders placed with suppliers that carry the Trade Assurance symbol, and the protection applies only when you pay online through Alibaba.com: a payment made outside the platform is not covered. If the product quality or the ship date differs from what you and the supplier agreed in the Trade Assurance online order, Alibaba says it will help reach a resolution, which can include a refund. You can request a refund within 30 days of delivery, the claim rests on your online order and your communication records, and you should inspect the products promptly on delivery (Alibaba.com, Trade Assurance for buyers). The page also says Trade Assurance is free for buyers.

  • Pay on the platform. A supplier who asks you to move the payment to a personal account or a bank transfer outside the order is asking you to give up the protection.
  • Write the specification into the order, not only into the chat. The order is the basis of any claim.
  • Check the goods the day they arrive, and keep photos and messages, because the 30 days run from delivery.

Trade Assurance is help with a dispute, not a guarantee, and it says nothing about who the supplier really is. Our Is Alibaba trustworthy? article covers what the badges do and do not tell you.

When Does a Letter of Credit Make Sense?

A letter of credit, which ICC calls a documentary credit, is a payment arrangement under ICC's UCP 600 rules. The buyer (the applicant) asks a bank to issue the credit in favour of the seller (the beneficiary); a second bank can add its own undertaking by confirming it. The bank pays when the seller makes a complying presentation, meaning documents that match the terms of the credit, the rules and international standard banking practice. The credit works independently of the sales contract, and once issued it cannot be amended or cancelled without the agreement of the issuing bank, the confirming bank if there is one, and the beneficiary (ICC Academy).

What that means for a buyer is general good practice rather than something the sources promise: because the bank examines documents, the credit protects you only as far as the documents you require, for example a bill of lading issued after the goods are loaded (see what to check on the bill of lading before you pay the balance) or an inspection certificate from an inspector you chose. It costs bank fees and paperwork, so it is usually discussed for larger or newer relationships; ask your bank for the cost and the steps.

What About PayPal and Cards?

Alibaba's buyer page lists several ways to pay inside the platform, including cards, PayPal, wire transfer and Apple Pay in one place and a longer list in the FAQ, and tells buyers to check the payment page for what is available to them. It adds that, for PayPal, protection starts only after you complete the online payment on Alibaba.com (Alibaba.com). The point to keep: the protection comes from paying inside the order on the platform, not from the brand of the payment method. Paying a supplier directly by PayPal or card outside an Alibaba order is a different transaction; the page does not describe protection for it.

How Should You Split the Payments?

This section is general advice, not a rule from the sources. A common arrangement is a deposit before production and the balance later; the useful part is what triggers each payment. Write it on the PI: for example, the deposit when the PI and the specification are confirmed, and the balance after an inspection report you have approved, or after you have checked the draft shipping document (what an inspection costs; what to check on the bill of lading before you pay the balance). Pay only to an account whose name matches the company on the PI and the business licence, and treat a change of bank details as a stop sign. The 10 things to check before paying a deposit covers the other checks.

What Does the Payment Method Not Tell You?

It does not tell you that the supplier is a manufacturer, that the company exists as described or that the goods will match the sample. A protected checkout can reduce the damage of a failed order; it does not reduce the chance of one. The checks in the China Supplier Verification Checklist and the 7 questions to ask before wiring money to an Alibaba supplier come before the payment, and an inspection comes before the balance. This is general information, not legal or financial advice: your bank can tell you what each payment route costs and what time limits apply.

What Should You Do Next?

  1. Choose the route before you send anything: an online order on Alibaba.com for suppliers that offer Trade Assurance, a direct transfer only for a supplier you have verified, a letter of credit if the order and the relationship justify the cost.
  2. Put the specification, the price basis and the payment triggers in the order or the PI, not only in the chat.
  3. Check that the beneficiary name matches the company you verified, and ask for the reason in writing if it does not.
  4. Tie the balance to an event: an approved inspection report or a checked shipping document, not to a date.
  5. Keep the evidence: the order, the messages, the PI, the payment confirmation and the photos from the day the goods arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to pay a Chinese supplier by bank transfer?

A bank transfer (T/T) is common, but it gives you the least built-in protection: once the money arrives the supplier has it, and your recourse is the contract, your written messages and a bank recall that has to be requested quickly. Use it for a supplier you have verified, pay only to an account in the company's name, and tie each payment to an event written on the PI.

Does Alibaba Trade Assurance protect me if I pay by bank transfer outside the platform?

No. Alibaba's buyer page says the protection applies only when you pay online through Alibaba.com, and that payments made outside the platform are not covered. Ask the supplier to put the order and the payment on the platform.

How long do I have to ask for a refund under Trade Assurance?

Alibaba's buyer page says you can request a refund within 30 days of delivery, and that the claim rests on your online order and your communication records. It also advises inspecting the products promptly on delivery.

What is a letter of credit, and do small buyers use it?

A letter of credit, called a documentary credit by ICC, is an arrangement in which a bank pays the seller against documents that comply with the credit's terms. It costs bank fees and paperwork, so ask your bank about the cost and steps before deciding whether the order justifies it.

Can I pay a Chinese supplier by PayPal or card?

Inside an Alibaba order, Alibaba's page lists cards and PayPal among the payment options, and says PayPal protection starts only after you complete the online payment on Alibaba.com. A direct PayPal or card payment outside an order is a different transaction, and the page does not describe protection for it.

Summary and Next Step

The payment method does not make a supplier trustworthy, but it decides how much you can recover and how quickly you find out. Pay inside an Alibaba order when the supplier offers it, write the specification and the payment triggers into the order, and use a direct transfer only for a supplier you have already verified. A letter of credit is worth its cost when the order is large enough and you can name the documents that matter.

In short, choose the route first and check the supplier before the money moves. Tell us about your order if you want help choosing how to pay.
