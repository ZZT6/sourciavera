DPI vs PSI vs Container Loading Inspection in China: When to Book Each

Quality Control
SourciaVera TeamNov 18, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: Choose DPI at 20% to 50% completion to catch factory defects early, and PSI at 80% to 100% packed before paying the balance. Add Container Loading Supervision (CLS) at loading to verify carton counts, container condition, and seal numbers under your contract.

What Is During Production Inspection (DPI) and When Does It Apply?

During Production Inspection (DPI) takes place inside the factory when 20% to 50% of the purchase order has come off the production line. The inspector checks semi-finished components, early finished units, assembly steps, and production speed against your approved sample and delivery schedule.

On an order of 2,500 units under ISO 2859-1 General Inspection Level II, the code letter is K and the standard sample size is 125 units. Detecting a dimensional tolerance drift or wrong finish when 30% of the lot is complete lets the factory rework 750 units and correct tooling before running the remaining 1,750 units.

  • Trigger point: 20% to 50% of units manufactured, with initial packaging materials already delivered to the workshop.
  • Primary checks: Raw material verification, dimensional measurements against CAD drawings, inline workmanship, and realistic completion date versus vessel booking.
  • When DPI matters most: First-time orders with a new factory, custom molds, or industrial equipment and auto parts with tight fitment tolerances.

What Is Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI) Under ISO 2859-1?

Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI) happens when 100% of units are manufactured and at least 80% are packed into export cartons, before you release the final 70% balance payment. Inspectors pull random cartons across pallets using Acceptable Quality Limit (AQL)—the maximum number of defective units allowed in a random sample before a lot fails—under ISO 2859-1 (ANSI/ASQ Z1.4).

For a 5,000-unit lot at General Level II (code letter L), the sample size is 200 units. Under standard trade thresholds—0 for critical defects, 2.5 for major defects, and 4.0 for minor defects—the lot fails at 1 critical defect, 11 major defects, or 15 minor defects. You can calculate exact sample counts and arrow-rule adjustments for your order using our ISO 2859-1 AQL sample size calculator.

  • Trigger point: 100% produced, 80% to 100% packed, at least 3 to 5 days before container pickup so rework remains possible.
  • Primary checks: Quantity count, visual appearance, functional testing, carton drop or barcode scan checks, and shipping marks matching the commercial invoice.

What Does Container Loading Supervision (CLS) Check at the Dock?

Container Loading Supervision (CLS) covers the physical transfer of approved cartons from the factory warehouse into a 20GP (approximately 28 to 33 CBM usable volume) or 40HQ (approximately 68 to 76 CBM usable volume) ocean container. Even after a lot passes PSI, factories can load damp containers, substitute older stock, or miscount master cartons.

During CLS, the inspector conducts a 7-point container structure check (roof light leaks, floor dryness, door gaskets, odor, and structural walls), counts 100% of loaded cartons against the packing list, verifies heavy cartons sit at the base to prevent crushing, and records the bolt seal number on camera when the doors lock.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should an importer book a During Production Inspection (DPI) in China?

Book a DPI when 20% to 50% of the order is finished and packed. At that stage, enough production exists to measure dimensional drift, material substitution, or assembly errors while the factory still has time to correct the remaining 50% to 80% without missing the vessel cutoff.

What percentage of goods must be packed before a Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI)?

Industry practice under ISO 2859-1 requires 100% of units to be manufactured and at least 80% packed into export cartons before a PSI. Inspecting earlier prevents random sampling across the full lot and allows unpacked units to bypass defect checks.

Does Container Loading Supervision (CLS) replace a Pre-Shipment Inspection?

No. CLS checks container dryness, floor integrity, carton count, stacking stability, and bolt seal numbers during loading. It does not open 80 to 315 sample units for dimensional or functional testing, so buyers pair PSI with CLS on full-container orders.
