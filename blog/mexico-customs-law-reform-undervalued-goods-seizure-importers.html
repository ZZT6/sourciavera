Mexico's Customs Law Reform: Goods Declared Below Their Value Can Be Seized. What Importers From China Should Do Now

Market News
SourciaVera TeamOct 17, 2026 · Updated Oct 24, 2026 · 10 min read

The short answer: Mexico published its Customs Law reform in the Diario Oficial on October 23, 2026, and it takes effect on January 1, 2027. It removes the 50% threshold for seizing goods declared below their value; rules due by December 31, 2026 set the goods and the procedure. Make sure your invoice shows the price you actually paid.

Verified as of October 24, 2026 against the decree published in the Diario Oficial on October 23, 2026. The vote counts and the figure of 2,541 operations come from the press.

This article covers what the reports say, what it means for a buyer's documents, and where a third-party check helps. It is based on two Mexican press reports and general good practice, not on any first-hand experience of these seizures. Ask your customs broker how the final rules apply to your goods.

Why Does the Reform Matter to an Importer Buying From China?

Three reasons, all about the invoice and the timing:

  • The old safety margin is gone. Before the reform, the law allowed a precautionary seizure only when the presumed undervaluation was above 50%. Senator Miguel Ángel Yunes Márquez, who chairs the Senate finance committee, said that this requirement limits the authority's ability to act on operations with relevant differences that do not reach that percentage, and the reform removes it (Onexpo).
  • Who decides the percentage is not yet clear. The reform leaves it to administrative rules to define which goods, which procedure and, in some cases, which percentages apply. The opposition called this discretion; the ruling party said the rules will be published before the law takes effect.
  • A cheap price becomes a documentation question. A senator for the opposition warned that buying cheaper could become a reason for suspicion, and the committee chair said that a difference in value is not enough by itself to prove an infraction (La Razón). Either way, the burden of showing why the price is real falls on whoever has the paperwork.

What Did Mexico's Senate Approve?

According to La Razón de México, the Senate approved on October 13, 2026 a reform of the Customs Law that widens the power to seize goods declared at a value lower than their real one and toughens penalties for infractions involving hydrocarbons. The bill passed in general with 66 votes for, 37 against and one abstention, and in particular with 66 for and 34 against (La Razón). The decree was then published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación on October 23, 2026 and takes effect on January 1, 2027 (DOF; Onexpo).

Onexpo also reports the main changes in the text. The 50% threshold is eliminated. When the presumed undervaluation is above 20%, the importer can replace the seizure only by depositing in cash the taxes that would be due on the difference between the declared value and the value the authority considers for similar goods. Below 20%, the importer can replace it with a deposit in a customs guarantee account. If the outcome is favourable to the importer, the guarantee is released or the money returned. For hydrocarbons and petroleum products, fines of 50% to 100% of the commercial value apply to infractions in documents, declarations and electronic transmission of information.

We read the decree in the Diario Oficial: the January 1, 2027 date, the 20% line that decides how a seizure can be replaced (article 154) and the fines of 50% to 100% for hydrocarbons (article 185) match the reports. The vote counts and the figure of 2,541 operations come from the press. Read the customs agency's rules once they exist before relying on a number.

What Is Still Not Known?

Quite a lot. The decree's second transitory provision says the customs authorities must publish in the Diario Oficial, no later than December 31, 2026, the rules that set the goods and the procedure for the new seizure ground, and may set the percentage of declared value against transaction value for other goods (DOF). The opposition raised this point, saying that the authority will decide by administrative rules whom to seize and at what percentage, and warned that the rules could mean delays, more storage costs and liquidity problems for small and medium companies (La Razón). Nothing in the reports says that goods from China are treated differently, or that any product is singled out.

On scale, a senator cited by La Razón said that between 2025 and August 2026 there were 2,541 operations that escaped a precautionary seizure for not reaching the 50% gap, with an estimated commercial value of 1,586 million pesos. The same report states that this figure does not equal evaded taxes and does not prove crimes.

What Should a Buyer Keep in Writing?

This section is general advice, not a rule from the reform. The aim is that, if customs questions a value, you can answer with documents.

  • An invoice that shows what you actually paid. The commercial invoice, the proforma invoice and the bank transfer should tell the same story. Do not accept a request to write a lower value on the invoice to reduce duties: a document that does not match the payment is a problem on its own (see how to pay a Chinese supplier).
  • The reason for a low price. If the price is low for a legitimate reason, such as volume, a standard factory price compared with a retail one or a clearance lot, have that reason in writing before shipment: the quotation, the agreed discount, the cost sheet if the supplier shares it.
  • A clear price term. The value customs looks at depends on what the price includes. Write the Incoterm and place on the PI (see which price term to ask a Chinese supplier to quote) so the declared value is not a guess.
  • A written description of the goods. Part number, material and specification. The authority compares your goods with similar goods, and a vague description is easier to question.
  • A customs broker who has seen the PI before the shipment leaves. The reform leaves the details to rules not yet published, so ask the broker what they expect to need and at what point they will confirm it.

What Does the Reform Not Tell You?

It does not tell you what customs will consider a normal price for your product, what percentage will trigger a seizure, or how long goods may be held. It does not say that every low-priced shipment will be seized: the committee chair said that a difference in value alone does not prove an infraction. And it says nothing about a particular supplier.

What Should You Do Next?

  1. Ask your customs broker how the reform affects your product and when the agency's rules will be published.
  2. Make the PI, the commercial invoice and the payment match, with a clear price term and a description of the goods.
  3. Write down why the price is what it is if it is well below the market.
  4. Check the supplier before you pay, especially when the price is far below other quotes.
  5. Leave time and cash for a possible delay on shipments that arrive after January 1, 2027. Mexico's separate electronic value declaration also has new dates: see the MVE dates and what to line up with your supplier.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did Mexico's Senate change in the Customs Law?

The Senate approved on October 13, 2026 a reform that removes the 50% threshold for a precautionary seizure of goods declared below their value. The decree was published in the Diario Oficial on October 23, 2026 and takes effect on January 1, 2027.

At what price gap will customs seize goods?

That is not set in the law. The decree leaves it to rules that the customs authorities must publish in the Diario Oficial by December 31, 2026, so ask your customs broker for them.

Does the reform apply only to goods from China?

The reports we read do not single out any country or product. They describe goods declared at a value below the one the authority considers for similar goods.

Can I release seized goods by paying a guarantee?

Under article 154 of the decree, if the declared value is 20% or more below the transaction value of identical or similar goods, the importer can replace the seizure only with a cash deposit of the taxes due on the difference; below 20% a deposit in a customs guarantee account is also possible. Check the rules with your broker.

What should the supplier's invoice show?

The price actually paid, with a clear price term, a description of the goods and the same company name and amount as the payment. Do not agree to write a lower value on the invoice to reduce duties.

Summary and Next Step

Mexico's Customs Law reform, published on October 23, 2026, removes the 50% threshold for a precautionary seizure of goods declared below their value. It takes effect on January 1, 2027, and the rules that set the goods and the procedure are due by December 31, 2026. For an importer buying from China, the safest preparation is documents that agree with each other and a price that you can explain.
