Product Inspection Cost Before Shipment: What Actually Determines the Real Price

Pre-Shipment Inspection
SourciaVera TeamJul 24, 2026 · Updated Oct 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Why comparing inspection quotes by day rate misleads you

Most buyers compare inspection quotes by the price per man-day. That's the wrong number. Two quotes at the same day rate can cover completely different amounts of work — one man-day checking 50 cartons isn't the same job as one man-day checking 500. A simple appearance check, a functional test, a multi-SKU inspection, and a re-inspection after corrections are not the same work, even when they're quoted at the same daily rate.

What you should be comparing is scope. And before that, it helps to separate inspection from factory verification: verification confirms the supplier exists and has the capacity they claim; inspection checks the specific product that's about to leave.

The short answer: the real cost of a pre-shipment product inspection is the day rate multiplied by the scope — how many SKUs and cartons get opened, which tests are run, and whether a re-inspection is included if the lot fails. A typical scenario: two "one man-day" quotes that weren't the same

The problem: it's a common pattern. A buyer with a finished order collects two inspection quotes. Both say "one man-day." Neither says how many SKUs will be sampled, which functions will be tested, or what happens if the lot fails and needs a second visit.

The outcome: the buyer picks the cheaper one, and only when the report arrives realizes half the SKUs weren't opened.

The takeaway: my advice is to nail down SKU count, carton count, and which defects you'd call critical versus minor before you ask anyone for a number.

Quick checklist: how to compare inspection quotes

  • Define inspection scope before comparing prices between service providers.
  • Have your SKU list, test points, carton count, and exact factory address ready.
  • Ask what the report includes: photos, video, sampling level, defect classification.
  • Budget time and cost for a re-inspection if the product needs correction.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as factory verification?

No. Verification checks the supplier before production — confirming the company exists, has real capacity, and isn't a middleman. Inspection checks the finished product before it leaves the factory for shipment. They are two separate stages of the same order.

Is a lower daily rate always better?

No. A cheaper inspection may skip SKU coverage, test depth, adequate photography, or time for re-inspection. The comparison should be made by scope, not just daily price.

Do I need an inspection on repeat orders with the same supplier?

Often yes — especially if the material, color, packaging, or factory workload changed. A reliable supplier on the last order may be subcontracting part of the process on the next one.

In my experience, disappointment with an inspection almost never comes from the inspector being bad. It comes from nobody agreeing, in writing, what the inspector was supposed to check.
