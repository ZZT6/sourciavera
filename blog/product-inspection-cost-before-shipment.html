Product Inspection Cost Before Shipment: What Actually Determines the Real Price

By Zhang Peng · August 29, 2026 · 5 min read

What you should define before comparing inspection quotes

Use this guide to turn an inspection quote into a decision with a clear scope — not a simple price-per-day comparison. Start by separating this from factory verification: verification confirms the supplier exists and has the declared capacity; inspection checks the specific product before it leaves the factory. Both matter, but they answer different questions. Define the number of SKUs, carton count, and what defects you'd consider critical — before asking for a price.

A simple appearance check, a functional test, a multi-SKU inspection, and a re-inspection after corrections are not the same work — even when quoted at the same daily rate.

Quick answer

  • Define inspection scope before comparing prices between service providers.
  • Have your SKU list, test points, carton count, and exact factory address ready.
  • Ask what the report includes: photos, video, sampling level, defect classification.
  • Budget time and cost for a re-inspection if the product needs correction.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as factory verification?

No. Verification checks the supplier before production — confirming the company exists, has real capacity, and isn't a middleman. Inspection checks the finished product before it leaves the factory for shipment. They are two separate stages of the same order.

Is a lower daily rate always better?

No. A cheaper inspection may skip SKU coverage, test depth, adequate photography, or time for re-inspection. The comparison should be made by scope, not just daily price.

Do I need an inspection on repeat orders with the same supplier?

Often yes — especially if the material, color, packaging, or factory workload changed. A reliable supplier on the last order may be subcontracting part of the process on the next one.
