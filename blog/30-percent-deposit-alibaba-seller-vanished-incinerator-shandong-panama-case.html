30% Deposit Paid, Alibaba Seller Vanished: A Panama Case

Case Study
SourciaVera TeamNov 9, 2026 · 9 min read

The short answer: A Panamanian businessman paid a 30% deposit for an incinerator in Shandong and his seller stopped answering. The reason was simple: the seller was on wedding leave. I traveled 1,700 km, filmed unedited video and checked the payment account before the balance was paid.

The client is 69 years old, with decades in medical waste treatment and half a century of working with boilers. He knows the equipment and he knows business. I publish this case with his permission and without the names of the client or the factory.

A Situation That Would Scare Anyone

A few months earlier he had found an incinerator factory in Shandong, China, on Alibaba. They agreed on the price and, following the platform's process, he paid a 30% deposit. Then things started to go wrong:

  • The salesperson he had always talked to stopped answering messages and calls.
  • Another person at the company asked him to pay the balance on Made-in-China.com instead of Alibaba.

He had already paid the deposit, his contact had disappeared and he was being asked to pay the remaining 70% on another platform. Anyone would worry; it is completely reasonable. In his place, I would already have refused to pay the balance.

He did not walk away. He wanted to get to know the factory better and give it a chance, because he understood that, for a small factory, his order could mean a lot. The one thing he did not know was what was really happening in China. At that moment he found me on the internet.

My Role: Helping Him See the Truth

I verify factories and do bilingual sourcing for buyers in Latin America. In this case my role was clear: not to decide for the client, but to help him see the truth. Being a bridge between China and Panama, and part of the foundation of trust between both sides, made me very happy.

First, a Phone Call: the Truth About the "Missing" Salesperson

With all the client's information, I first contacted the factory manager. He explained that the salesperson had gotten married: during his wedding leave he could not answer at all times, and from Panama it looked as if he had "disappeared". About the switch to Made-in-China.com for the payment, the manager also gave me an explanation. I took the payment account certificate they sent me and compared it point by point with the platform's information.

I recorded every point of the conversation, organized it and sent it to the client:

  • Does the factory exist? Yes. It is in Liaocheng, Shandong, and it is a real factory.
  • Why did the salesperson disappear? He got married and was on leave. He had not run away.
  • Is the payment account reliable? The certificate matches the platform's information.
  • Where is the equipment? At the factory, and it can be seen in person.
  • Freight and container type? There is a formal quote from the freight forwarder. The equipment fits in a 40HC container.

Half of the weight came off the client's shoulders. The other half had to be seen with his own eyes.

Two Details in the Spec Sheet, Solved Before Traveling

Before leaving I went through the equipment configuration list with the client, point by point, and we found two details that could cause problems:

  • The burner power. The sheet said 0.35 kW and the client was alarmed: how can it burn anything with so little? When we asked the factory we learned that 0.35 kW is the power of the fan motor. The burner is 200,000 kcal/h, about 233 kW. A misunderstood number can end in a big misunderstanding.
  • The fuel. In Panama the client will use LPG (liquefied petroleum gas), but the machine came with a diesel tank. We had to agree with the factory, before the trip, on changing the burner to LPG.

From Guangzhou in Short Sleeves to the Cold of Shandong

On inspection day I left Guangzhou early: 3 hours by plane and another 2 hours by taxi. In Guangzhou people were still in short sleeves. In Liaocheng the wind cut across my face.

The client did not ask for a video call. He gave me a list of what he wanted to see:

  • A full walk around the machine.
  • A person standing next to the machine, to see its real size.
  • The refractory lining inside the two combustion chambers.
  • The burner and its nameplate.
  • The control panel powered on.
  • The burner igniting, recorded without a single cut.

I uploaded each video as an unlisted link and sent them to him. For the burner video I used a simple title: "Real video, unedited".

The Twist: the Factory Wanted to Sell Two Machines

When I arrived I found two incinerators joined together. The client was only going to buy one at first; the second, when he had the money in December. The factory owner's concern was direct: if they separate the machines and the client takes the first, what happens if he later does not want the second? That is why the factory wanted a deposit for the second machine, as a guarantee.

That was the key point of everything, and I told the client clearly: "What the factory really wants is a deposit for the second machine. Maybe it is part of their sales strategy, but what they are looking for is a guarantee." The client was also honest: the money for the second one arrives in December, he cannot leave a deposit yet, but he commits to buying it.

The final solution was practical:

  • Each machine uses its own control panel and ignites separately.
  • At the end the smoke outlets are joined into one, to do a single emissions test as if it were one unit.
  • The first one ships first; the second, in December.

In the client's words: "This is the practical solution."

Why Has He Not Paid the Balance Yet?

When the inspection ended, the client watched the videos and the first thing he said was: "The work has been very good." The second: "When should we pay?" I asked him to wait one more night:

  • We had to confirm with the factory whether the switch to LPG changed the price.
  • We had to confirm the payment account again the next day.

I told him: "It is better to be safe with that amount of money." Someone who lived through the "disappearing salesperson" does not need someone who pushes him to pay. He needs someone who guards the last door for him.

An Unexpected Advantage: Two Machines in the Same Container

The client had also ordered a shredder from a factory in Zhengzhou. Because it was being made for Panama's voltage, it would not arrive in time for the original ship and would have had to go as separate consolidated cargo. The incinerator was delayed by a week because of the switch to LPG and the preparation of the electrical panel, and the dates lined up. The shredder is sent directly to Liaocheng and leaves with the incinerator in the same container. The client saves an entire consolidated freight charge.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if the seller stops answering after I pay a deposit?

Contact the factory through another channel, such as the manager, and ask for confirmation in writing. In this case the salesperson was on wedding leave. Then verify that the factory exists, the payment account and the equipment before you pay the balance.

Is it a sign of a scam when they ask me to pay the balance on another platform?

Not by itself, but it is a reason to verify. Ask for the payment account certificate and compare it point by point with the platform's data, and confirm the account again on the day of payment.

What should I ask for in a machinery verification video?

A full walk around the equipment, a person standing next to it to show its real size, the inside of the chambers, the burner and its nameplate, the control panel powered on and the ignition recorded without cuts.

Summary and Next Step

After watching the video, the client wrote: "The equipment looks very robust. It is like buying an anvil." At 10 at night I told him I was going to rest, and he answered: "You keep banker's hours!" I replied: "A minister of intelligence spends a lot of neurons negotiating with the factories. They have to be let to rest so that tomorrow they keep working for the empire."

Looking back, the misunderstanding was not complicated: a salesperson got married and a payment platform changed. But for a client on the other side of the world, every change without an explanation is a reason not to pay. What I did was simple: call, take notes, verify accounts, take a plane, a taxi and stand in the cold wind recording videos.

You can compete on prices. You cannot compete on trust. I flew 1,700 km to be able to say a single sentence: "Relax, I saw it with my own eyes."
