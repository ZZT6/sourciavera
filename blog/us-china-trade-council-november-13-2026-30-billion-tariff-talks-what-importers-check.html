US-China Trade Council Talks: What Importers Check Now

Market News
SourciaVera TeamNov 15, 2026 · Updated Nov 23, 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer: The U.S.-China Trade Council held its first formal call on November 13, 2026, without announcing any new tariff deal. The November 18-19 APEC Shenzhen summit closed with leaders' declarations and bilateral talks, leaving the $30B-for-$30B tariff framework unfinalized and the trade pause through January 10, 2027 in place.

Verified as of November 15, 2026 against Lianhe Zaobao (summarizing China's Ministry of Commerce announcement of November 14, 2026, Reuters, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's CNBC interview) and apec.org for summit dates. We did not open a separate USTR press release, so U.S. side details are attributed to the cited reports.

This article sets out what negotiators discussed on November 13, 2026, what remains unfinalized before the November 18-19 APEC summit, and what U.S. buyers can ask suppliers and customs brokers to confirm. It is based on the cited reports and general sourcing practice, not first-hand participation in the talks. Your licensed customs broker decides how tariff rules apply to your entries.

According to China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) statement on November 14, 2026, as reported by Lianhe Zaobao, the video call followed up on earlier consultation outcomes, including the September round, covering tariffs, the "$30 billion for $30 billion" reciprocal tariff-reduction arrangement, agricultural trade, and other concerns. Under that $30 billion framework, negotiators have discussed lowering duties toward most-favored-nation rates on roughly 90% of covered goods on each side, though neither government announced a final implementation date after the November 13 call.

The pause keeps existing Section 301 exclusions—temporary tariff exemptions granted for specific product classifications—and port-fee suspensions in focus, yet buyers should not assume lower duties are active until official notices are published. For the earlier timeline, see the trade truce extension through January 10, 2027 and the November 6 trade call.

Why Is U.S. Export-Control and Transshipment Scrutiny Rising?

  • Treasury warning on third-country routing. In a November 13 CNBC interview cited by Lianhe Zaobao, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent criticized the Commerce Department for not adding Singapore-based AI firm Megaspeed to the Entity List, noting that Treasury, Defense, State, and USTR supported listing it, and questioned whether chipmakers adequately screen customers to prevent third-country transshipment.
  • Congressional push ahead of NDAA. Chairs of three U.S. House committees sent a letter requesting a closed-door briefing on November 18, 2026, and urged Congress to attach advanced-chip security and export-control bills to the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) (Lianhe Zaobao).
  • What this means for dual-use items. Dual-use items are goods, software, or components with both civilian and military applications that face strict export licensing. Even if headline tariff talks stay constructive before Shenzhen, compliance checks on electronics, industrial equipment, and third-country routing are tightening rather than easing.

What Is Still Unknown?

  • Whether the $30B-for-$30B tariff cuts will be finalized after the November 18-19 APEC Shenzhen summit or which exact Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) lines would be included.
  • When domestic procedures for the January 10, 2027 truce will be published in formal U.S. Federal Register notices.
  • Whether Megaspeed or additional intermediaries will be added to the U.S. Entity List, or which export-control provisions will enter the final NDAA.
  • Any USTR readout of the November 13 call. Our summary relies on Lianhe Zaobao's report of MOFCOM's statement and cross-checked trade coverage.

What Should a U.S. Buyer Confirm Before Placing or Shipping Orders?

This section is general sourcing practice, not a rule from the sources.

  • Verify your product's exact HTS code and active duty rate with your customs broker rather than pricing orders on unfinalized "$30B-for-$30B" reductions (see Section 301 exclusions status).
  • Confirm origin and routing documents so shipments do not rely on unverified third-country re-invoicing or vague factory declarations.
  • Lock tariff-change responsibility in writing on your proforma invoice before wiring a deposit (see what to check on a proforma invoice).
  • Vet new suppliers directly at the factory level instead of relying on trading intermediaries (see the supplier verification checklist).

What Should You Do Next?

  1. Audit open POs scheduled to clear U.S. customs between November 2026 and January 10, 2027.
  2. Ask your customs broker to confirm current Section 301 and reciprocal duty rates for each SKU.
  3. Document supplier origin and verify business licenses before paying deposits on new factories.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did the November 13, 2026 U.S.-China Trade Council call result in a new tariff agreement?

No. According to China's Ministry of Commerce as reported by Lianhe Zaobao on November 14, 2026, both sides held candid and constructive talks on tariffs, the $30B-for-$30B cut framework, and farm trade, but announced no new agreement or effective date.

Is the U.S.-China trade truce extension through January 10, 2027 already finalized?

Both sides agreed to extend the truce until January 10, 2027, but Lianhe Zaobao reports they still must complete domestic procedures to finalize specific execution arrangements.

What is the $30 billion for $30 billion tariff arrangement?

It is a reciprocal tariff-reduction framework under discussion in the Trade Council covering about $30 billion in goods on each side, with proposals to lower tariffs toward most-favored-nation rates on roughly 90% of covered items.

Why is export-control scrutiny tightening ahead of APEC in Shenzhen?

On November 13, 2026, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent criticized the lack of an Entity List designation for Singapore firm Megaspeed and questioned third-country transshipment screening, while House committee chairs pushed to include chip export-control bills in the NDAA.

Summary and Next Step

The November 13, 2026 U.S.-China Trade Council call kept talks moving on tariffs and the $30B-for-$30B framework without announcing a signed agreement, and the November 18-19 APEC Shenzhen summit closed without a new permanent tariff-cut notice. Keep your landed-cost models tied to published rates ahead of the January 10, 2027 truce expiration.
