What Are the Procedures to Buy Goods From a Factory or Company Wholesale for Retailing Purposes?

Sep 7, 2026 · 10 min read · Zhang Peng, Founder
Back to Blog

Many beginners think wholesale purchasing is simple:

Find a factory→Pay money→Receive products→Sell them

But in reality, successful wholesale buying requires much more preparation. The biggest mistake new retailers make is focusing only on the product price. A low purchase price does not guarantee a profitable business. You need to consider supplier selection, product verification, cost calculation, and quality control — together, not just the last line on a quote. Here are the 8 steps that actually make up the procedure.

1. Define Your Product Requirements Before Contacting Suppliers

Before searching for factories, you should clearly understand what you need. Important details include:

  • Product specifications
  • Materials
  • Size and color options
  • Packaging requirements
  • Certifications (if needed)
  • Target selling price

For example, saying "I want to buy water bottles" is not enough. A factory needs to know: stainless steel or plastic? What capacity? Custom logo? Individual packaging or bulk packaging? The more specific you are, the easier it is to find the right supplier — and the harder it is for a vague or underqualified factory to talk you into whatever they happen to already have in stock.

2. Find and Evaluate Potential Suppliers

There are many ways to find suppliers: industry exhibitions, B2B platforms, factory websites, professional sourcing partners. But finding suppliers is the easy part. The difficult part is determining: can this supplier actually deliver what they promise? A beautiful online page does not always represent a reliable manufacturer — four things separate the two, and none of them are visible in the listing photos:

What You CheckReal, Independent FactoryTrading Company / Middleman
Production floorOwns a dedicated production floor, typically 5,000–50,000+ m², with the relevant equipment on-siteRents a small office or showroom; production is subcontracted elsewhere
Business license scopeRegistered manufacturing scope matches the product categoryRegistered as trading/wholesale, or the scope doesn't cover the product at all
Export experienceCan produce shipping records or client references for your product categoryVague answers about "many clients," no verifiable export history
Live video requestWilling to film the actual line, a dated sign, and today's output on requestSends a pre-recorded tour, or the video "can't be arranged this week"

3. Order Samples and Confirm Product Quality

Before placing a large wholesale order, always test samples. Check product quality, material consistency, packaging, functionality, and appearance. Many problems happen because buyers approve products based only on pictures. A sample gives you a realistic understanding of what your customers will receive — and it becomes the physical reference you compare the bulk shipment against later, which is worth nothing if you don't keep it and document it in writing.

4. Calculate Your Real Wholesale Cost

Do not only calculate the factory price. A product that looks cheap at the factory may become expensive after reaching your warehouse. Your real cost typically includes:

  • Product cost
  • Custom packaging
  • Domestic transportation (factory to port)
  • Inspection fees
  • Shipping cost (ocean or air)
  • Import duties
  • Taxes

As a rough planning guide based on the landed-cost breakdowns we build for clients across different product categories, the fully landed cost of an imported product typically runs 1.3–1.6× the factory FOB price once packaging, freight, inspection, and duties are added — the exact multiplier depends heavily on your product category and destination country's duty rate. Always calculate your own landed cost before you quote a retail price, not after the container has shipped.

5. Negotiate Order Terms and Confirm Details

Before production starts, confirm everything in writing: price, quantity, production time, payment terms, quality standards, packaging requirements, and shipping terms. Common international trade terms include EXW, FOB, CIF, and DDP — each shifts a different point in the journey (and a different set of costs and risks) from the seller to you. Clear agreements prevent misunderstandings later — and how you structure payment is where risk control actually gets decided:

Payment MethodRisk LevelWhen It's Used
100% T/T upfrontHigh risk — avoidNo leverage if goods don't match spec; common ask from unverified sellers

6. Control Quality Before Shipment

One of the biggest mistakes new importers make is checking quality only after receiving the goods. By then, it may already be too late. Professional buyers usually arrange production monitoring, factory inspection, and pre-shipment inspection — it is much cheaper to solve problems before shipping than after the products arrive. Watch for these patterns across the whole relationship, not just at the inspection stage:

  • Refuses a factory visit or live video — the single biggest red flag
  • No verifiable certifications for a product category that requires them
  • Pricing 20–30%+ below every other quote, with no material explanation
  • Pressures you to skip inspection or pay 100% before shipment
  • Reuses stock or third-party photos instead of dated originals
  • Gives vague, evasive answers to specific technical questions
  • Has no verifiable reference clients or export history
  • Credibility rests entirely on the website, with no physical evidence behind it

None of these red flags are visible from a listing page. They only surface when someone is physically checking — which is exactly the gap between a retailer who finds a supplier and one who verifies a supplier before wiring the balance.

Final Thoughts

Buying wholesale from a factory is not just about finding the cheapest supplier. The real process is:

Define requirements→Find suppliers→Verify factories→Test samples→Calculate costs→Confirm terms→Control quality→Ship

Many successful retailers understand that their biggest advantage is not only the product they sell. It is their ability to build a reliable supply chain behind that product. Because one bad supplier can destroy months of marketing, sales, and customer trust.
