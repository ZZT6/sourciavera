The Problem

Why One Audit Isn't Enough

An initial audit confirms the factory exists and has real capacity. It doesn't guarantee that actual production, weeks later, matches what was agreed.

The most costly problems — undisclosed material substitutions, unreported subcontracting, quality defects — usually show up during production, not before it.

That's why "supervising" a supplier in China means something different from simply verifying them once: it means having someone on the ground, in person, throughout the entire process.

Key Difference

One-Time Audit vs. Continuous Supervision

An audit answers "can they manufacture what they promise?" Supervision answers "are they manufacturing what we agreed on, right now?"

When We Step In

The 4 Critical Checkpoints of Supervision

Production errors are cheap to fix early — and very expensive to fix at the end. That's why we define specific control points on every order.

Stage 01

Raw Material Arrival

We verify the materials entering the line match the agreed specifications — composition, gauge, origin. This is the first point where undisclosed substitutions happen, and the cheapest to correct before the material is processed.

Stage 02

First Article

We review the first unit produced before mass production starts. If there's a mold, spec, or assembly error, it can be corrected here without the cost of reworking the entire batch.

Stage 03

Production Midpoint

Sample review at 50% of the order already manufactured. Confirms consistency between the start and midpoint of production — catches cumulative quality drift before it affects the full order.

Stage 04

Pre-Container Loading

Final check before the order leaves the factory: unit count, packaging integrity, labeling, and confirmation that what goes into the container matches the agreed order exactly.
