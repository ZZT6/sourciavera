Import Glossary: Mexican and US Customs Terms

Updated Oct 28, 2026 · 25 terms · Zhang Peng (Miguel Lopez), Founder

The short answer: this glossary defines the Mexican and US customs terms our import articles use. It covers pedimento, MVE, VUTCE, E15, antidumping duties, de minimis, Entry Types, AQL, man-day, importer of record, CAPE and China’s export VAT rebate. Each definition cites a text linked below it: an official one for customs terms, our own pages for AQL and man-day.

Customs terms verified as of October 28, 2026 against the official texts linked under each one. AQL and man-day follow our own pages, not an official text. “Ultimate consignee” is not included yet because we could not tie it to a text we could read. This page is general information, not legal advice.

Pedimento
The electronic customs entry for a shipment. Anyone who brings goods into or out of Mexico under a customs regime must transmit one to customs through the electronic customs system, with information about the goods.Source: Ley Aduanera, art. 36
MVE (Manifestación de Valor Electrónica)
The statement, made under oath, of the elements that let customs determine the customs value of the goods, transmitted electronically. From November 1, 2026 it becomes mandatory in stages by customs regime, and for definitive imports from January 15, 2027, according to the SAT's advance text of the Third Resolution, which we did not find in the Diario Oficial as of October 24, 2026.Sources: Ley Aduanera, art. 59, fracción III; SAT, Tercera Resolución, primera versión anticipada
VUTCE (Ventanilla Única de Trámites de Comercio Exterior)
The Single Window for Foreign Trade Procedures, enabled by a presidential decree published on May 4, 2026 as the only channel through which the foreign trade authorities (the Ministry of Economy, the SAT and the National Customs Agency) receive and resolve procedures. The Digital Transformation Agency (ATDT) runs it. The RGCE call it the "Ventanilla Digital".Sources: DOF, decreto del 4 de mayo de 2026; DOF, Segunda Resolución RGCE 2026 (22 de septiembre)
VUCEM (Ventanilla Digital Mexicana de Comercio Exterior)
The system through which, according to the same decree, the VUTCE operates technologically. Its administration is to pass from the SAT to the ATDT. So VUTCE is the window and VUCEM is the system behind it, not a renaming.Source: DOF, decreto del 4 de mayo de 2026, transitorio tercero
E2 (Manifestación de Valor)
The customs value declaration format of the RGCE annexes. The E15 contract information is transmitted together with the E2 that corresponds to it.Source: DOF, Segunda Resolución RGCE 2026, Anexo 1
E15 (Información de contratos asociados a la Manifestación de Valor)
A format in Annex 1 with the general data of the contracts behind a transaction. Until December 31, 2026, importers may transmit it, declared under oath, instead of each contract.Source: DOF, Segunda Resolución RGCE 2026 (22 de septiembre)
Agente aduanal (customs broker)
A natural person authorized by the Finance Ministry, through a patent, to handle the clearance of goods on behalf of others under the customs regimes. The broker is responsible for the accuracy of the data, the correct tariff classification and the determination of the regime.Source: Ley Aduanera, arts. 54 y 159
Cuota compensatoria (antidumping or countervailing duty)
A duty that the Ministry of Economy sets on imports sold at dumped prices or subsidized. It equals the difference between normal value and export price, or the amount of the subsidy; it can be lower if that is enough to discourage the unfair practice.Source: Ley de Comercio Exterior, art. 62
Compromiso de precios (price undertaking)
An agreement in which exporters commit to sell at no less than a minimum price, with the duty not applied to the companies that comply. In the ceramic tile case, the 2016 Final Resolution accepted a commitment of at least USD 6.72 per m2 FOB or 8.40 CIF, with price monitoring before export.Source: DOF, resolución de inicio del examen de vigencia (23 de octubre de 2026)
Examen de vigencia (sunset review)
Mexican antidumping duties disappear after five years unless the Ministry has started a review before then. To start one, domestic producers must state their interest in writing at least 25 days before the duty expires. In the ceramic tile case the duties stay in force while the review runs.Sources: Ley de Comercio Exterior, arts. 70 y 70 B; DOF, resolución del 23 de octubre de 2026, punto 34
De minimis
The US exemption from duty for shipments with a fair retail value of USD 800 or less. It has been suspended for most imports since August 29, 2025 and indefinitely by CBP rules since June 24, 2026, and the statute ends it on July 1, 2027, according to CBP's proposed rule.Source: Federal Register, 91 FR 64532 (8 de octubre de 2026)
Entry Type 11 (informal entry)
The CBP entry type for goods valued at USD 2,500 or less. CBP proposes to require it to be filed electronically on or before the date of importation instead of within 15 days after arrival.Source: Federal Register, 91 FR 64532 (propuesta)
Entry Type 13 (informal mail entry)
A new entry type that CBP proposes for mail shipments of USD 2,500 or less. CBP began a voluntary test of it on September 22, 2026.Source: Federal Register, 91 FR 64532 (propuesta)
Entry Type 86
A voluntary electronic entry process for qualifying low-value shipments in CBP's ACE system. CBP says the test proved insufficient for the problems it was designed to address.Source: Federal Register, 91 FR 64532
Liquidated damages
The amount CBP claims when an importer breaks a bond condition. For the informal entry bond, CBP proposes the greater of the value of the goods or USD 1,000, and three times the value for restricted or prohibited goods or alcohol.Source: Federal Register, 91 FR 64532 (propuesta de 19 CFR 113.62(n))
AQL (Acceptance Quality Limit)
The agreed limit on defects in an inspection sample. In ISO 2859-1 it is the worst quality level still considered acceptable as a process average when lots are inspected one after another; in day-to-day trade it works as the pass-or-fail limit, the number of defects a sample may contain before the lot is rejected. Common starting points are 0 for critical, 2.5 for major and 4.0 for minor defects, and the buyer can set others. An AQL sample is a statistical check, not a guarantee. This entry follows our Inspection Standards page, not the text of the standard.Source: SourciaVera, Inspection Standards (based on ISO 2859-1 and ANSI/ASQ Z1.4)
Man-day
One inspector working one day at the factory. An order with many models or a large quantity may need more than one man-day. Our pre-shipment inspection is priced per man-day, so two quotes at the same day rate can cover different amounts of work. This entry follows our own pages, not an official text.Sources: SourciaVera, Inspection Standards (services section); SourciaVera, China inspection companies compared (FAQ)
Importer of record (IOR)
The party that has the right to make entry of goods into the United States: the owner or purchaser of the goods or, when designated by the owner, purchaser or consignee, a licensed customs broker. A nominal consignee may not make entry on its own behalf.Source: CBP, Customs Directive 3530-002A, section 5.1
CAPE (IEEPA refund tool)
Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries: the function in CBP's ACE system through which importers of record and the customs brokers who filed the entries submit declarations to request refunds of IEEPA duties, consolidated rather than entry by entry. CBP adds it in phases.Sources: CBP, IEEPA Duty Refunds; CBP, IEEPA Duty Refunds fact sheet, April 2026
Export VAT rebate (China)
A refund, set for each product by China's tax authorities, of part of the value-added tax included in the cost of goods that are exported. The rate that applies is set by the export date on the customs declaration. When the rate falls, the exporter recovers less, and that difference becomes a cost for the exporter.Sources: Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration, Announcement No. 2 of 2026 (extract reproduced by the Hong Kong Trade and Industry Department); KPMG China, China Tax Alert, October 2026
Precautionary seizure (embargo precautorio)
A measure by which Mexican customs holds goods while an administrative procedure decides whether there was an infraction. The person concerned can present documents to rebut it (Customs Law art. 153) or ask to replace the seizure with a deposit or guarantee (art. 154), as amended by the decree published on October 23, 2026.Source: DOF, Decreto por el que se reforman y adicionan diversas disposiciones de la Ley Aduanera, 23/10/2026
Transaction value (valor de transacción)
The price paid for the imported goods, adjusted with certain charges, which is normally the customs value in Mexico. The price paid is the total payment the importer has made or will make, directly or indirectly, to the seller or for the seller's benefit.Source: Ley Aduanera, arts. 64 y 65 (PDF oficial de la Cámara de Diputados)
Antidumping duty (AD)
In the US, a duty that CBP assesses, on Commerce's instruction, equal to the amount by which the normal value of the goods exceeds their export price, once Commerce finds the goods sold at less than fair value and the ITC finds a US industry materially injured.Source: Commerce, Federal Register notice 91 FR 67815 (October 28, 2026)
Countervailing duty (CVD)
In the US, a duty that CBP assesses, on Commerce's instruction, based on the net countervailable subsidy rate set for the exporter, to offset a foreign government subsidy, once the ITC finds a US industry materially injured by the subsidized imports.Source: Commerce, Federal Register notice 91 FR 67815 (October 28, 2026)
Cash deposit (AD/CVD)
The estimated antidumping or countervailing duty that CBP requires the importer to deposit at the same time as the importer would normally deposit estimated customs duties. The duty actually assessed is set later on Commerce's instruction to CBP.Source: Commerce, Federal Register notice 91 FR 67815 (October 28, 2026)
