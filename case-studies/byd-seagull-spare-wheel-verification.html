Verifying BYD Seagull spare wheels before purchase.

Zhang Peng, founder of SourciaVera (Guangzhou). Based on our own sourcing work for this client, sourcing wheels for Chinese EV models sold in Latin America.

1. Customer requirement

The client needed a complete spare wheel kit for the BYD Seagull: a steel wheel, tire, and the hardware to actually use it in an emergency — jack, lug wrench and storage bag — not just the bare rim. The specification that matters most on a spare wheel is fitment: offset (ET), center bore (CB) and bolt pattern (PCD) all have to match the vehicle exactly, because a spare is meant to go on without a second thought.

2. Supplier quotation

Our team contacted and evaluated four wheel factories on the client's behalf. All four quoted a price; not all four could back up their numbers:

  • Several factories confirmed the offset verbally only, with no way to verify it before shipment.
  • One supplier's business license and Alibaba listing gave an address in Chengdu's Jinniu District — a district known more for sales offices than production floors.
  • One factory, in Sichuan, was willing to issue real manufacturing data in writing before the client committed.

The factory that ultimately won the order sent its first spec sheet listing the offset as ET 54.1 — a number that looked wrong immediately, because 54.1 mm is the vehicle's center bore, not its offset.

3. China-side investigation

Before accepting any quotation, we checked what a listing page can't show:

  • Registered vs. actual address. For the Chengdu supplier, we asked to see the real production line — it turned out to be in Hongguang, across the city from the registered/sales address. Not automatically a scam, but not what the listing said either.
  • Written re-verification. We refused to accept the winning factory's first spec sheet as final and required a formal, stamped specification document.
  • Fitment proof. We required an actual video of the wheel installed on the vehicle, showing it seated without a spacer.

4. The spec issue we found

Wheel / tire15×3.5 steel / 125/80R15Confirmed correct

A 24 mm gap between the two figures, on a part where a few millimeters already changes suspension geometry, tire wear and how the car handles. If a spacer had been used to compensate for the wrong offset as documented, it would have affected steering geometry, cornering and tire wear — exactly the kind of problem a buyer discovers only after the parts land.

5. Final recommendation

We recommended proceeding with a full sample kit order — wheel, tire, jack, lug wrench and storage bag together, exactly as the end user would receive it — through Alibaba Trade Assurance, conditional on the corrected, stamped specification. We would not have recommended proceeding on the first, unverified spec sheet.

6. Result

The sample shipped and reached the client in September. After testing it against the vehicle, the client confirmed it met the requirement, and the conversation has moved to a follow-up bulk order.

What this case shows about sourcing wheels from China

Even the factory that manufactures the part can mix up its own paperwork. A verbal confirmation, or a spec sheet nobody pushed back on, is not verification. Based on this and other factory visits, we now ask every wheel factory for a written, stamped specification before any sample order — regardless of how confident the factory sounds on the phone.
